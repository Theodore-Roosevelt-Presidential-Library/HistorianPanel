# Gilded Age New York

Read `reference/panel-rules.md` at the plugin root first.

## Scope

- The Roosevelt family in New York City: houses, streets, neighborhoods,
  churches, clubs, and where events happened.
- Theodore Roosevelt Sr.'s civic and philanthropic work and the founding
  history of the institutions he supported.
- Tammany Hall, city and state machine politics, police corruption and the
  reform movements T.R. met.
- Society, wealth and the social world of the Roosevelts and their circle.
- New York during the Civil War as it touched the family.

## Out of scope

- T.R. as Police Commissioner and Civil Service Commissioner →
  `sp-civil-service-police`
- The Albany assembly years → `sp-albany-assembly`
- Family offices, careers and life events → `sp-family-offices`
- Lineage and cousins → `sp-generations`
- Sagamore Hill and the birthplace as sites → `sp-roosevelt-sites` or
  `sp-nps-sites`
- Quotes → `sp-quote-verifier`

## Where to look first

1. **Institutional records and finding aids.** The Children's Aid Society
   records at the New York Historical (finding aid at
   findingaids.library.nyu.edu/nyhs/ms111_childrens_aid_society/) and other
   finding aids at findingaids.library.nyu.edu. Founding dates and founders
   come from the institution's own records, not from family biographies.
2. **New York Historical, Patricia D. Klingenstein Library**
   (nyhistory.org/library): manuscripts, directories, maps and society
   ephemera.
3. **New York Public Library:** Digital Collections
   (digitalcollections.nypl.org) for city directories, photographs and
   ephemera; the NYPL Map Warper (maps.nypl.org/warper) for historical maps
   and atlases. Directories give who lived where in a given year; streets were
   renumbered, so match the map to the year.
4. **NYC Municipal Archives** (archives.nyc): vital records, building and tax
   records, and city government files.
5. **Museum of the City of New York collections** (collections.mcny.org) for
   photographs of streets and buildings, with catalog dates.
6. **Period newspapers in the original pages:** Chronicling America
   (chroniclingamerica.loc.gov) and NYS Historic Newspapers
   (nyshistoricnewspapers.org). Check the issue date against the event date.
7. **NPS Theodore Roosevelt Birthplace National Historic Site**
   (nps.gov/thrb), history pages with notes. Secondary for context.
8. **Secondary syntheses:** Edwin G. Burrows and Mike Wallace, *Gotham*
   (1998); David McCullough, *Mornings on Horseback* (1981); Edward P. Kohn,
   *Heir to the Empire City* (2014); Richard Zacks, *Island of Vice* (2012).
   Secondary; follow their notes.

## Known traps

- A family member is credited with founding an institution he supported,
  funded or served. Check the founding record for who founded it and when.
  [ref: B-10]
- Civil War-era family decisions (service, substitutes, commutation) are told
  in a simplified or wrong form. Find the legal mechanism used and a record of
  it before repeating the story. [ref: B-12]
- A Washington or New York address is attached to the wrong family member or
  the wrong years. Confirm in a directory or deed for that year. [ref: B-15]
- Party or faction labels are put on officials who did not hold them, for
  example calling someone a machine loyalist without evidence. Check the
  official record and period reporting. [ref: F-23]
- Corruption charges against a named official are stated as fact when they
  rest on testimony or rumor. Cite the investigation report and say what it
  found. [ref: F-24]
- Street numbers and neighborhood names changed over the century. A
  present-day address or name is applied to an earlier date. [method]
- "Society" claims (who attended, who was received) come from gossip columns
  or later memoirs. Label them as reported. [method]
- A biography's portrait of the city is used as evidence for a specific
  address, date or founding. Go to the record it cites. [method]

## Procedure

1. Pin the claim to a year, a place and an institution or person.
2. For an address, check a city directory or map for that year, then a second
   record (deed, census, letter heading, period newspaper).
3. For an institution, find its own founding records or finding aid, and a
   second independent account.
4. For a political claim, find the official record (investigation report,
   election return, appointment notice) and period reporting.
5. Check whether names of streets, neighborhoods or institutions have changed.
6. Write the narrowest correction, keeping roles exact (founder, trustee,
   donor, volunteer).
