# Museum specimens and expedition records

Read `reference/panel-rules.md` at the plugin root first.

## Scope

- Specimens collected by T.R., Kermit and other members of his expeditions
  (boyhood and college collecting, the African expedition, the South American
  expedition, and others).
- Which institution sponsored an expedition and received its specimens.
- Specimen counts, game lists and catalog numbers.
- Expedition records: correspondence, field catalogs, photographs and reports
  held by museums and their archives.
- Whether a specimen is in a research collection or on public exhibit, now or
  in the past.

## Out of scope

- The expeditions as narrative events → `sp-africa-europe-bull-moose`,
  `sp-last-years`
- Hunting and natural history as a theme of his life → `sp-natural-history`
- Who is in an expedition photograph → `sp-photo-id`
- Objects that are not specimens → `sp-objects`
- Indigenous people, lands and cultural material encountered on expeditions →
  the Library's human reviewers

## Where to look first

1. **Smithsonian Institution Archives**, https://siarchives.si.edu/ . Records of
   any expedition the Smithsonian sponsored or joined, with related
   correspondence, accession records and photographs. Best for sponsorship and what was accessioned.
2. **National Museum of Natural History collection databases**,
   https://collections.nmnh.si.edu/ , and the Smithsonian Collections Search
   Center, https://collections.si.edu/ . Specimen records with catalog numbers,
   collector and locality. Collector fields can be inconsistent; search name
   variants and the expedition name.
3. **American Museum of Natural History**: Research Library and archives
   (https://www.amnh.org/research/research-library) and the AMNH Library
   Digital Repository (https://digitallibrary.amnh.org/). Records of AMNH
   involvement in T.R.-era expeditions, and museum publications.
4. **Museum of Comparative Zoology, MCZbase**, https://mczbase.mcz.harvard.edu/ .
   Harvard's specimen database, including material from T.R.'s youth and
   college years.
5. **T.R.'s own expedition books** with their appendices: *African Game Trails*
   (1910) and *Through the Brazilian Wilderness* (1914), read in scans of the
   first editions (see `sp-published-works`). Appendices list game and
   specimens; they are the expedition's own account.
6. **Biodiversity Heritage Library**, https://www.biodiversitylibrary.org/ ,
   for museum reports, scientific descriptions of expedition specimens and
   Smithsonian annual reports. **GBIF**, https://www.gbif.org/ , aggregates
   museum specimen records and can find a specimen across institutions.

## Known traps

- Game-list figures are copied with transposed or invented numbers, or animals
  shot by different members of the party are combined. Read the list in the
  first edition and note whose column each figure comes from. [method]
- One institution's role is credited to another (an expedition sponsored by
  one museum described as collecting for a second). Check the sponsoring
  institution's own records. [method]
- "Collected for the museum" and "on exhibit at the museum" are different
  claims. Most specimens sit in research collections; exhibit claims need a
  dated exhibit record or museum statement. [method]
- Specimen counts differ by what is counted (individual specimens, lots, skins
  and skeletons separately, all collectors or T.R. alone). Say what the source
  counts. [method]
- Collector fields in databases credit the expedition, a field naturalist, or
  "Roosevelt" without saying which Roosevelt. Read the full record and accession
  data before crediting T.R. personally. [method]
- Specimens were exchanged, transferred or deaccessioned over the decades.
  The current holder may not be the original recipient. [method]
- Rounded figures in later popular accounts get restated as exact. Go to the
  museum report or the expedition book. [method]
- Common and scientific names have changed since T.R.'s day. Match on the
  record, not on a modern name alone. [method]

## Procedure

1. Pin down the claim: which expedition or period, which collector, which
   institution, which specimen or count, and whether it is about collection,
   accession or exhibit.
2. Check the expedition's own published account and appendices in a first
   edition.
3. Check the receiving institution's archives for sponsorship, accession and
   correspondence records.
4. Search the specimen databases for catalog records, using collector and
   expedition name variants; note catalog numbers.
5. For counts, state what is counted and by whom, and compute any totals
   yourself in `notes`.
6. For exhibit claims, find a dated exhibit record or museum statement; if none,
   limit the claim to the collection.
7. Cite with institution, collection, catalog or accession number, and URL.
