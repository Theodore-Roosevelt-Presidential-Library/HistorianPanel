# Foreign policy and diplomacy

Read `reference/panel-rules.md` at the plugin root first.

## Scope

- Crises and settlements: the Venezuela crisis, the Alaska boundary, Santo
  Domingo customs, the Roosevelt Corollary, Morocco and Algeciras.
- Panama: the revolution, any U.S. naval presence, recognition, the
  Hay-Bunau-Varilla Treaty and canal construction as diplomacy.
- Japan and Russia: T.R.'s role in the Russo-Japanese War settlement, the Treaty of
  Portsmouth and its terms, the Taft-Katsura memorandum, the Gentlemen's
  Agreement, Root-Takahira.
- Arbitration and the Hague conferences.
- The Nobel Peace Prize: award year, reasons, presentation and any
  lecture.
- Diplomatic posts and ranks of U.S. representatives abroad and foreign
  envoys in Washington.

## Out of scope

- The navy as an instrument (fleet routes, ships, the war of 1898) →
  `sp-military-naval`
- Order of Secretaries of State and other cabinet officers →
  `sp-officeholder-sequences`
- Biographies of Hay, Root, Taft, Spring Rice and Jusserand as people →
  `sp-political-literary-circle`
- Quotations, including famous lines about Panama or the "big stick" →
  `sp-quote-verifier` (also routed here for occasion and date)
- "First American to..." claims → `sp-superlatives`
- Effects on Indigenous peoples or on living people → the Library's human
  reviewers

## Where to look first

1. **Office of the Historian, U.S. Department of State
   (history.state.gov).** The Milestones essays for 1899-1913
   (history.state.gov/milestones/1899-1913) give dated overviews; the
   *Foreign Relations of the United States* (FRUS) volumes
   (history.state.gov/historicaldocuments) print the cables and notes. FRUS
   is primary; Milestones is a secondary summary that cites it.
2. **Office of the Historian chiefs-of-mission and people lists
   (history.state.gov/departmenthistory/people/chiefsofmission).** The
   authority for each envoy's rank, post and dates of appointment and
   presentation of credentials.
3. **Treaty texts.** Bevans, *Treaties and Other International Agreements of
   the United States of America, 1776-1949*, on the Library of Congress Law
   Library site (loc.gov/collections/united-states-treaties-and-other-
   international-agreements), and the Avalon Project at Yale
   (avalon.law.yale.edu). Use for terms and signature dates; check which
   version (signed, ratified, proclaimed) you are citing.
4. **U.S. Senate treaty records (senate.gov/about/powers-procedures/
   treaties.htm)** and the Senate Executive Journal. For advice-and-consent
   and ratification dates, and for agreements that were never treaties.
5. **The American Presidency Project (presidency.ucsb.edu).** Annual
   messages to Congress and special messages on Panama and Santo Domingo.
   Find the message in which a policy was first stated rather than assuming
   the year.
6. **NobelPrize.org (nobelprize.org/prizes/peace/1906/roosevelt/facts/).**
   Award facts and the Nobel lecture text and date.
7. **T.R.'s letters (Library of Congress T.R. Papers; Theodore Roosevelt
   Center scans) and *An Autobiography* (gutenberg.org/ebooks/3335).**
   Primary for his account; written to justify, so pair with FRUS.

## Known traps

- "Ambassador" is used for envoys who held the rank of minister. Many posts
  were legations rather than embassies at some dates. Check the rank in the
  chiefs-of-mission record for that post and year. [ref: G-15, E-07]
- Proposals made during a negotiation are reported as the final terms, or
  the final terms are reported as T.R.'s proposal. Separate what was
  proposed, by whom, from what the treaty says. [method]
- The award year, ceremony and lecture of the Nobel Peace Prize are merged,
  or the prize is dated relative to the events it recognized. Use the Nobel record and
  give each its own date. [ref: G-01]
- Naval events in the Panama revolution are compressed into a single
  dramatic statement ("the navy prevented a landing"). Read the FRUS cables
  for what each ship did and when. [method]
- Agreements, memoranda and exchanges of notes (Gentlemen's Agreement,
  Root-Takahira, Taft-Katsura) are mislabeled as treaties, or the reverse.
  Name the instrument type from the document itself. [method]
- Signature, ratification and proclamation dates are used interchangeably.
  Say which date you are giving. [method]
- Famous lines about Panama are quoted in a smoothed-up version. Send wording to `sp-quote-verifier`; check the venue and year
  here. [method]
- A policy is credited to T.R. alone when a Secretary of State or envoy
  drafted or negotiated it. Name who did what, from the documents. [method]
- Later outcomes (canal completion, the fleet's role in Asia) are read back
  as the reason for an earlier decision. Keep cause and sequence to what the
  dated record shows. [ref: G-11]

## Procedure

1. Identify the instrument, crisis or post, and the exact date the claim is
   about.
2. Read the FRUS document or treaty text for that date. Note whether it is a
   treaty, an agreement, a memorandum or an exchange of notes.
3. For any diplomat, check rank, post and dates in the chiefs-of-mission
   record.
4. Add an independent second source: a Senate record, a presidential
   message, T.R.'s own letter, or a scholarly account citing the documents.
   Milestones and a secondary work that cites Milestones count as one.
5. Separate proposal from outcome, and signature from ratification.
6. Hand off naval operations, cabinet sequences, biographies and quotes as
   listed above.
7. Write the narrowest accurate correction.
