# Political and literary circle

Read `reference/panel-rules.md` at the plugin root first.

## Scope

- Identity and careers of people in T.R.'s political, diplomatic, literary
  and scientific circle: names, life dates, education, professions, books.
- Offices they held, with exact titles and dates, as they bear on a claim
  about the person.
- How and when each met T.R., and what each relationship was (mentor,
  collaborator, correspondent, appointee, rival) at a given date.
- Breaks and reconciliations between T.R. and members of the circle.
- Friends' published accounts of T.R., as evidence of the friend's view.

## Out of scope

- Who held an office before or after whom, and the order of officeholders →
  `sp-officeholder-sequences` (also routed here for the person)
- Quotations by or about circle members, including famous remarks attributed
  to Hanna, Lodge or Wister → `sp-quote-verifier`
- The Dakota circle (Sewall, Dow, Ferris, Merrifield, de Mores) →
  `sp-badlands-circle`
- Family members → `sp-family-offices`; Edith → `sp-edith`
- The substance of conservation policy and acreage → `sp-conservation`
- Treaty terms and negotiations → `sp-foreign-policy`
- Claims about living people → the Library's human reviewers

## Where to look first

1. **Official office lists.** Biographical Directory of the U.S. Congress
   (bioguide.congress.gov) for senators and representatives; Office of the
   Historian lists of Secretaries of State and chiefs of mission
   (history.state.gov/departmenthistory/people); the Federal Judicial
   Center's biographical directory of federal judges (fjc.gov/history/judges).
   Authoritative for titles and dates; they do not describe relationships.
2. **T.R.'s letters.** Library of Congress T.R. Papers
   (loc.gov/collections/theodore-roosevelt-papers) and Theodore Roosevelt
   Center scans (theodorerooseveltcenter.org). The best evidence for what a
   relationship was at a given date. Read the dated letter itself.
3. **The friends' own papers.** For example the Henry Cabot Lodge Papers at
   the Massachusetts Historical Society (masshist.org), John Muir's
   correspondence at the University of the Pacific
   (scholarlycommons.pacific.edu/muir-correspondence), and Pinchot material
   described by the Forest History Society (foresthistory.org). Use finding
   aids to see what survives.
4. **Published correspondence.** *Selections from the Correspondence of
   Theodore Roosevelt and Henry Cabot Lodge* (1925) and other edited
   volumes. Primary texts, but editors cut letters; check the full letter
   when a claim turns on wording.
5. **Friends' memoirs and tributes.** Wister's *Roosevelt: The Story of a
   Friendship* (1930), Riis's writings, Burroughs's *Camping and Tramping
   with Roosevelt* (1907), Hagedorn's books. Evidence of the author's view,
   written after the fact; label recollections as recalled.
6. **Period newspapers, through Chronicling America
   (chroniclingamerica.loc.gov).** For public breaks, endorsements and
   appointments as reported at the time.
7. **Standard biographies of the friends** (modern lives of Lodge, Taft,
   Root, Hay and Pinchot). Secondary; use for leads and read the documents
   they cite. Name the author, title and year you used.

## Known traps

- A friend's job or title at the time of meeting T.R. is projected back from
  a later career (a man described by a post he held only later, or the
  reverse). Date the title to the year of the meeting. [method]
- Office tenures are given starting when T.R. became president, when the
  person was appointed earlier by another president. Check the start date in
  the official list. [ref: G-18]
- Titles that changed over time, or a commission title versus a later
  executive title, are mixed up. Use the exact title for the exact date.
  [ref: G-19]
- "Ambassador" is used for a diplomat who held the rank of minister. Check
  the chief-of-mission record for the rank. [ref: G-15, E-07]
- Lines attributed to friends and rivals are quoted as if documented when
  they come from later memoirs.
  Send wording to `sp-quote-verifier` and label it as recalled. [ref: D-18,
  F-07, F-11]
- A relationship is described as constant when it changed: early allies who
  later broke, or rivals who reconciled. Say which year you mean. [method]
- A friendship is dated from the first surviving letter, which may not be
  the first meeting. Say what the record shows. [method]
- Friends' memoirs are written as testimony and often flatter. Treat them as
  the author's view, not as independent proof of an event. [method]
- The Library's own pages, timelines and tools are not sources. [house-rule]

## Procedure

1. Identify the person exactly and the date the claim is about.
2. For any office, find the title and dates in the official list, and note
   who appointed the person.
3. For the relationship, find a dated letter or contemporary record that
   shows it at that date.
4. Add an independent second source: the friend's papers, a period
   newspaper, or a standard biography with the document it cites.
5. If the claim rests on a memoir, label it as recalled and narrow it.
6. Hand off sequences, quotes and policy substance as listed above.
7. Write the narrowest accurate correction.
