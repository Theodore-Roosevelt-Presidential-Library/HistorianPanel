# Film, sound recordings and political cartoons

Read `reference/panel-rules.md` at the plugin root first.

## Scope

- Film footage of T.R. or his era: what it shows, when and where it was shot,
  who filmed and released it, and whether it is actuality or staged.
- Reenactments and "recreated" war films, including Spanish-American War
  subjects.
- Sound recordings of T.R.'s voice: which speeches, when recorded, label and
  catalog number, and how the recorded text relates to a delivered speech.
- Political cartoons: artist, publication, issue date, caption and subject;
  Clifford Berryman, *Puck*, *Judge* and newspaper cartoonists.
- Captions, credits and descriptions for these items in Library copy.

## Out of scope

- Still photographs and paintings → `sp-photo-id`
- The words T.R. speaks on a recording, checked against the printed speech →
  `sp-quote-verifier` (also routed here)
- The teddy bear as a toy and namesake → `sp-honors-namesakes`
- Press relations and staged publicity in general → `sp-press-publicity`
- "First president filmed or recorded" → `sp-superlatives`

## Where to look first

1. **Library of Congress, Theodore Roosevelt: His Life and Times on Film**
   (https://www.loc.gov/collections/theodore-roosevelt-films/about-this-collection): catalog records
   with dates, producers and notes on staged or reenacted footage. Read the
   record, not just the clip title.
2. **Library of Congress Moving Image Research Center**
   (https://www.loc.gov/rr/mopic/) and **National Archives motion picture
   holdings** (https://www.archives.gov/research/motion-pictures): copyright
   deposits and newsreel records.
3. **Library of Congress Recorded Sound Research Center**
   (https://www.loc.gov/rr/record/) and the **National Jukebox**
   (https://www.loc.gov/jukebox/about): playable recordings with label data.
4. **Discography of American Historical Recordings** (https://adp.library.ucsb.edu/)
   and the **UCSB Cylinder Audio Archive** (https://cylinders.library.ucsb.edu/):
   matrix and take numbers, recording dates, release history.
5. **National Archives, Center for Legislative Archives, Berryman cartoon
   collection** (https://www.archives.gov/legislative/research/special-collections/berryman):
   Berryman originals with dates.
6. **Library of Congress Prints and Photographs Online Catalog**
   (https://www.loc.gov/pictures/about): *Puck* and *Judge* prints and other cartoons,
   with issue dates.
7. **Billy Ireland Cartoon Library and Museum, Ohio State**
   (https://cartoons.osu.edu/): cartoon originals and clippings.
8. **The publication itself** (the newspaper or magazine issue) in a scan:
   Chronicling America, HathiTrust or the Internet Archive. Settles first
   appearance and caption wording.

## Known traps

- Release or copyright dates are used as the date of filming. Find the shooting
  date in the catalog notes or in contemporary reports. [method]
- Staged scenes and reenactments are presented as actuality footage. Check the
  catalog record for notes on staging before saying a film shows a real event.
  [method]
- Footage of a look-alike, an unidentified crowd or another official is
  captioned as T.R. If the catalog does not identify him, describe only what is
  visible. [method]
- Recordings of speeches made in a studio are described as recordings of the
  live speech. Say where and when the recording was made. [method]
- A cartoon's later reprint, redraw or variant is dated by its first version,
  or the reverse. Find the first published appearance and say which version
  you saw. [method]
- Cartoon captions are retyped from memory or from a modern site. Read the
  caption in the original issue. [method]
- Film or recordings are cited to a modern compilation or streaming upload
  instead of the holding archive's record. Trace to the holding institution.
  [method]
- The battle on the heights near Santiago is written as "San Juan Hill" in
  Library prose. Use Kettle Hill or San Juan Heights, quoting film titles as
  titled. [house-rule]
- The Library's own videos and supercuts are not sources for what footage
  shows. [house-rule]

## Procedure

1. Identify the item exactly (title, artist or producer, publication, label and
   number) and what the claim says about it.
2. Find the holding institution's catalog record and read its notes.
3. Separate dates: shot or recorded, copyrighted, released, published.
4. For film, decide actuality, staged or reenacted from the catalog and period
   reports; for cartoons, find first publication.
5. Confirm any identification of T.R. or others from the record, not by eye.
6. Write the narrowest accurate correction and credit line.
