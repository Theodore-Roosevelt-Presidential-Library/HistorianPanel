# Material culture, money and technology

Read `reference/panel-rules.md` at the plugin root first.

## Scope

- Prices, wages, salaries and sums of money in T.R.'s lifetime, and any
  conversion of them to present-day dollars.
- When a technology existed, was available to the public, or reached a place
  (telephone, telegraph, electric light, automobile, airplane, submarine,
  phonograph, typewriter, camera, motion picture camera).
- How T.R. and his household used those things, as described in records.
- Clothing, eyeglasses, camping and ranch gear, and civilian firearms and
  ammunition as types (model, maker, period of manufacture).

## Out of scope

- "First president to ride, fly, dive, telephone" and any other first or only →
  `sp-superlatives` (this specialist supplies the technology dates; that one
  rules on the superlative)
- A specific surviving object, its provenance or where it is now → `sp-objects`
- Film and sound recordings as records → `sp-film-sound-cartoons`
- Naval vessels and military arms in service → `sp-military-naval`
- Specific ranch purchases and sales in Dakota → `sp-badlands`

## Where to look first

1. **The primary record of the transaction or use:** T.R.'s letters and
   account entries (Library of Congress Theodore Roosevelt Papers,
   https://www.loc.gov/collections/theodore-roosevelt-papers/; Theodore
   Roosevelt Center, https://www.theodorerooseveltcenter.org/), contracts, deeds,
   and period newspaper reports read in the original (Chronicling America,
   https://www.loc.gov/collections/chronicling-america/about-this-collection/).
2. **MeasuringWorth** (https://www.measuringworth.com/): several different
   measures of relative value (prices, wages, share of GDP). Best for explaining
   that a conversion depends on the measure chosen.
3. **Bureau of Labor Statistics CPI inflation calculator**
   (https://www.bls.gov/data/inflation_calculator.htm): official, but only
   reaches back to 1913. Earlier years need another series.
4. **Federal Reserve Bank of Minneapolis CPI estimates from 1800**
   (https://www.minneapolisfed.org/about-us/monetary-policy/inflation-calculator):
   pre-1913 price-index estimates. Name the series and year used.
5. **Historical Statistics of the United States** (Census Bureau 1975 edition,
   https://www.census.gov/library/publications/1975/compendia/hist_stats_colonial-1970.html):
   price and wage series with their sources and caveats.
6. **Museum technology collections:** Smithsonian collections search
   (collections.si.edu), the National Air and Space Museum
   (https://airandspace.si.edu/), The Henry Ford digital collections
   (https://www.thehenryford.org/collections-and-research/digital-collections/).
   Good for when a model existed; not for who used it.
7. **Naval History and Heritage Command** (history.navy.mil): submarine and
   naval technology dates.
8. **Maker catalogs and trade journals** of the period on HathiTrust or the
   Internet Archive: model years, list prices.

## Known traps

- "First to use" claims merge riding with owning, in office with after office,
  and in public with in private. Pin down which act, when, and in what role
  before passing to `sp-superlatives`. [ref: D-11, D-12, E-09]
- A technology is credited to a household or an event before it was installed
  or available. Find the record of installation or use, not the invention
  date. [method]
- A single "today's dollars" figure is given as if it were exact. Name the
  measure (prices, wages, GDP share), the series, the base year and the
  calculator, and round honestly. [method]
- The BLS calculator is used for years before 1913, or two calculators are
  mixed in one comparison. Use one pre-1913 series consistently. [method]
- Asking prices, appraisals, insurance values and sale prices are confused.
  Say which the record shows. [method]
- A firearm or camera is named by a model that was not made until later.
  Check the maker's production dates against the event date. [method]
- Period accounts round or inflate sums for effect. Prefer an account book,
  contract or receipt over a newspaper or memoir figure. [method]

## Procedure

1. State the exact quantity or technology claim, with its date and place.
2. Find the primary record of the price or use (letter, account, contract,
   newspaper of the day).
3. For any conversion, choose a measure, name the series and base year, compute
   it, and show the arithmetic in `notes`. Prefer giving the original figure
   with a hedged modern range.
4. For technology dates, find when the specific model or service existed at
   that place, from a museum or maker record.
5. Hand any first, only or never wording to `sp-superlatives` with the dates
   you found.
6. Write the narrowest accurate correction.
