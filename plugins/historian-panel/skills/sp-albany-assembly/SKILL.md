# Albany Assemblyman and the losses of February 1884

Read `reference/panel-rules.md` at the plugin root first.

## Scope

- T.R.'s nomination and elections to the New York State Assembly from his
  Manhattan district, and his three terms.
- Assembly work: investigations, reform bills, votes, committee posts, the
  Speaker contest, and his allies and opponents.
- The birth of his daughter Alice and the deaths of his mother and his first
  wife in February 1884: where, when, the recorded causes, and the funeral.
- The 1884 Republican National Convention: his role as a delegate, the
  nomination fight, and his decision about the ticket afterward.

## Out of scope

- His departure for and life in Dakota → `sp-badlands`
- Later life of daughter Alice → `sp-family-offices`; her own figure, once
  added → `sp-alice-longworth`
- Who held the Speakership or other offices, in order → 
  `sp-officeholder-sequences`
- Medical detail of the causes of death beyond what the record states →
  `sp-medicine-health`
- Quotes, including the diary page of February 1884 → `sp-quote-verifier`
  (also routed here)
- "Youngest" and "first" claims → `sp-superlatives`

## Where to look first

1. **Journal of the Assembly of the State of New York** for the 1882, 1883 and
   1884 sessions: the official record of votes, bills and committees. Read in
   scans at HathiTrust (https://www.hathitrust.org/) or the Internet Archive
   (https://archive.org/); the New York State Library
   (https://www.nysl.nysed.gov/) holds the series.
2. **New York State Archives** (https://www.archives.nysed.gov/): legislative
   and state records. Ask what survives before citing a bill file.
3. **T.R.'s diary and letters for 1882-1884:** Library of Congress, Theodore
   Roosevelt Papers (https://www.loc.gov/collections/theodore-roosevelt-papers/)
   and Theodore Roosevelt Center scans
   (https://www.theodorerooseveltcenter.org/digital-library/).
4. **NYC Municipal Archives** (https://www.archives.nyc/): city vital records,
   including death records, for place, date and recorded cause.
5. **Period newspapers, read on the page:** NYS Historic Newspapers
   (https://nyshistoricnewspapers.org/) and the Library of Congress's
   Chronicling America (https://www.loc.gov/collections/chronicling-america/).
   Fulton History (https://fultonhistory.com/) has many New York papers but is
   a private, OCR-only site; confirm the page image.
6. **Official proceedings of the 1884 Republican National Convention,** read in
   a scan at HathiTrust or the Internet Archive, for roll calls and speeches.
7. **Secondary, for leads only:** Edmund Morris, *The Rise of Theodore
   Roosevelt* (1979); Edward P. Kohn, *Heir to the Empire City* (2014).

## Known traps

- Details of the two deaths of February 1884 (order, hour, where in the
  house, interval after the birth) are embroidered past the record. Use the death
  records and the dated diary page, and keep each detail to what a source
  states. [ref: B-07]
- A cause of death is assumed from the circumstances (a recent birth, a
  shared illness) rather than taken from the record. Use the death record and
  an authoritative biography, and keep the birth and the cause separate. [ref: B-14]
- The newspaper report of a vote or speech is dated by its issue date rather
  than the session day. Check the Assembly Journal for the date. [method]
- Bills he introduced, bills he backed and bills that passed are blurred into
  "his law." Find the bill, its sponsor and its fate in the Journal. [method]
- Leadership titles are inflated (minority leader, Speaker candidate, chairman)
  or placed in the wrong session. Confirm each title for the specific year.
  [method]
- His convention role and his decision to support the ticket are compressed
  into one moment. Date each step from contemporary newspapers and letters.
  [method]
- The famous diary page from February 1884 is quoted from memory or from a
  poster. Read the scan. [method]
- The Library's own timelines and family trees are not independent sources.
  [house-rule]

## Procedure

1. Pin the claim to a session, date, place or vote.
2. For legislative claims, find the entry in the Assembly Journal for that
   session; then a newspaper report read on the page.
3. For the February 1884 events, find the vital records and the diary scan;
   compute every interval and check the day of the week.
4. For the convention, use the official proceedings and contemporary reports.
5. Prefer contemporary records to later memoirs, and note any disagreement.
6. Route medicine, offices-in-order and quotes to their specialists.
7. Write the narrowest accurate correction.
