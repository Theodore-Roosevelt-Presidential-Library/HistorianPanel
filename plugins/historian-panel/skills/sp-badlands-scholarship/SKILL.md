# Badlands and North Dakota scholarship

Read `reference/panel-rules.md` at the plugin root first.

## Scope

- Claims that cite, or plainly come from, the secondary literature on T.R. in
  Dakota: Hermann Hagedorn, *Roosevelt in the Bad Lands* (1921); Lincoln Lang,
  *Ranching with Roosevelt* (1926); Carleton Putnam, *Theodore Roosevelt: The
  Formative Years* (1958); Roger L. Di Silvestro, *Theodore Roosevelt in the
  Badlands* (2010); Clay S. Jenkinson's books on T.R. in Dakota; and the
  collection of T.R.'s Dakota speeches and writings edited by James F. Vivian,
  *The Romance of My Life* (1989). All secondary.
- Ray H. Mattison's articles in *North Dakota History* and other articles in
  that journal.
- National Park Service studies of Theodore Roosevelt National Park.
- Whether a Badlands anecdote rests on a contemporary record or on a
  reminiscence, and how much weight it can bear.

## Out of scope

- The underlying Badlands fact (date, place, ranch, person) → `sp-badlands`
  (also routed here)
- Ranching economics, the cattle trade and the wider open range →
  `sp-open-range-west`
- General T.R. biographies → `sp-biographies`
- Quoted words, including speech texts → `sp-quote-verifier`
- Indigenous history of the region → flag for the Library's human reviewers
  (panel rules section 10)

## Where to look first

1. **Documents the books reprint, read as documents.** Hagedorn reprints
   letters, diary entries and newspaper items; read them in a full scan
   (Project Gutenberg, gutenberg.org/ebooks/24317, or the Internet Archive) and
   then find the original where you can. The narrative around them is
   secondary.
2. **T.R.'s own Dakota record:** diaries and letters in the Library of
   Congress Theodore Roosevelt Papers (loc.gov) and the Theodore Roosevelt
   Center digital library (theodorerooseveltcenter.org), which is based at
   Dickinson State University.
3. **State Historical Society of North Dakota:** archives and manuscripts
   (history.nd.gov/archives), and the journal *North Dakota History*
   (history.nd.gov/publications/ndhistory.html) with its cumulative index
   (history.nd.gov/publications/cumulativeindex.html). Good for Mattison and
   for local records; journal articles are secondary.
4. **NPS park studies:** the Theodore Roosevelt National Park history pages
   (nps.gov/thro) and the park's document shelf at NPS History
   (npshistory.com/publications/thro/index.htm), which holds the historic
   resource study, the historic structures report, the Brooks and Mattison
   historic handbook, and extracts of Mattison's articles. Good syntheses with
   notes; check the notes.
5. **Period Dakota newspapers in the original pages:** Chronicling America
   (chroniclingamerica.loc.gov) and Digital Horizons
   (digitalhorizonsonline.org) for North Dakota collections. Not through any
   dataset the Library built from them.
6. **Vivian's speeches collection** for which Dakota speeches exist and where
   they were printed; then read the printing it names.

## Known traps

- Reminiscences gathered decades after the fact (by Hagedorn, Lang and others)
  are treated as contemporary evidence. Name who remembered, when they said
  it, and prefer a contemporary record when the two disagree. [method]
- A speech text reprinted in a secondary book from a period newspaper is
  smoothed into modern wording, or the reprint is cited instead of the
  newspaper. Go back to the printed report. [ref: E-13]
- A day-by-day episode the book narrates from a journal is compressed into one
  date. Read the journal entries the book is based on. [ref: E-18]
- Secondary accounts date the end of the ranching years by different
  milestones. Say which milestone the claim means. [ref: E-15, E-20]
- Later popular books repeat Hagedorn without saying so. Two such books are one
  source (panel rules section 2). [method]
- A line T.R. wrote in more than one form (a speech, a later letter) is blended
  in secondary retellings. Cite one version. [ref: D-26]
- The 1883 trip and the 1884 return are merged in retellings, so ages and
  circumstances are mixed. [ref: D-23]
- Events placed in Dakota actually happened across the line in Montana
  Territory. [ref: D-25]
- North Dakota landforms are buttes, never mesas, even when a secondary source
  says otherwise. [house-rule]
- The Library's own Badlands tools, timelines and datasets are not sources.
  [house-rule]

## Procedure

1. Identify which book, article or study the claim comes from. Find the
   passage.
2. Classify what the passage rests on: a reprinted document, T.R.'s own
   writing, a period newspaper, or a later reminiscence. Note who and when.
3. For a reprinted document, find the original or a faithful scan and read it
   there.
4. For a reminiscence, look for a contemporary record that confirms or
   contradicts it. If none exists, label the claim as recalled.
5. Check dates, days of the week and territory (Dakota or Montana).
6. Find a second source independent of the first book.
7. Write the narrowest correction, keeping attribution for recalled material.
