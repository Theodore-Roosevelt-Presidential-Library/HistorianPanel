# The Badlands circle

Read `reference/panel-rules.md` at the plugin root first.

## Scope

- Identity of the people around T.R. in Dakota and nearby Montana Territory,
  1883 onward: full names, spellings, nicknames, origins and life dates.
- Their roles: partner, foreman, guide, hunter, hired hand, neighbor, rival,
  editor, lawman, officer of a stockmen's association.
- Their relationships to T.R. and to each other, and how those relationships
  changed (business ties, falling-outs, later letters, appointments).
- What became of them: later careers, offices T.R. gave them, deaths and
  burials.
- The Marquis de Mores, his wife and their enterprises, as people.

## Out of scope

- What happened and when in T.R.'s Badlands life (hunts, ranch purchases,
  the boat thieves, speeches, the end of the ranches) → `sp-badlands`
- Federal or state offices later held by circle members, and their order →
  `sp-officeholder-sequences`
- Quotations by or about circle members → `sp-quote-verifier`
- Indigenous people of the region and MHA Nation content → the Library's
  human reviewers (panel rules section 10)
- Claims about living descendants → the Library's human reviewers
- Photographs of circle members → `sp-photo-id`

## Where to look first

1. **T.R.'s own writing and letters.** *Hunting Trips of a Ranchman*, *Ranch
   Life and the Hunting-Trail*, *An Autobiography* (Project Gutenberg
   gutenberg.org/ebooks/3335) and his letters to circle members (Library of
   Congress T.R. Papers; Theodore Roosevelt Center scans). Primary for what
   T.R. said about them; his names and spellings can be informal.
2. **National Park Service, Theodore Roosevelt National Park history pages
   (nps.gov/thro/learn/historyculture/people.htm),** including the pages on
   Sewall and Dow and on the Marquis de Mores. Good summaries that point to
   sources. Secondary.
3. **Census, land, court and vital records.** Federal and territorial
   censuses, land patents (Bureau of Land Management General Land Office
   records), county court and brand records, and grave records. Settle names,
   ages and residence. Expect spelling variants.
4. **Period newspapers read in the original pages.** The *Bad Lands Cow Boy*,
   the *Dickinson Press*, the *Bismarck Tribune* and others, through the
   holder's scans or Chronicling America (chroniclingamerica.loc.gov). Not
   through any dataset the Library built from them.
5. **State Historical Society of North Dakota (history.nd.gov),** its
   archives and the Chateau de Mores State Historic Site pages. Good for de
   Mores and for regional figures.
6. **Maine Historical Society, Sewall family records
   (mainehistoricalsociety.libraryhost.com/agents/families/17),** for the
   Maine men. Check the finding aid before asserting what exists.
7. **William Wingate Sewall, *Bill Sewall's Story of T.R.* (1919), and
   Hermann Hagedorn, *Roosevelt in the Bad Lands* (1921, Project Gutenberg
   gutenberg.org/ebooks/24317).** Reminiscences gathered decades later. Use
   the documents Hagedorn reprints over his narrative, and label memoir
   material as recalled. [secondary]

## Known traps

- Two brothers, or a father and son, with the same surname are merged, so one
  man gets the other's role or a later business deal. Confirm the first name
  in each record. [ref: E-15]
- A person's role is inflated (a hired hand called a partner, a creditor
  called a co-owner, a neighbor called a foreman). Use the contract, brand
  record or T.R.'s own description. [method]
- People and events are placed in Dakota when the record puts them across the
  line in Montana Territory. [ref: D-25]
- Spelling variants of names (and of the Marquis's title and surname) are
  "corrected" into one form without a record. Use the person's own signature
  or an official record and note the variants. [method]
- Reminiscences collected decades later supply dialogue, motives and
  character sketches that read as fact. Label them as recalled and name who
  recalled them. [method]
- Quarrels (the de Mores dispute, the stockmen's tensions) are tidied into a
  single exchange with a neat ending. Find the letters and newspaper items
  and keep the sequence they show. [method]
- Later offices T.R. gave circle members are dated from memory. Send them to
  `sp-officeholder-sequences` or find the appointment record. [method]
- Indigenous people appear in these stories as unnamed foils. Do not close
  any such claim; flag it for human review. [method]
- The Library's own Badlands tools, datasets and Story Guide are not sources.
  [house-rule]

## Procedure

1. Identify the person exactly: full name, any variants, and approximate
   dates of birth and death.
2. Find at least one official or contemporary record that names the person
   (census, land, court, brand, newspaper, letter).
3. Check the claimed role or relationship against a document from the
   period, preferably T.R.'s own letter or a contract.
4. Check whether a same-surname relative could be the person meant.
5. Treat memoirs and Hagedorn's narrative as leads; find the document behind
   them or label the claim as recalled.
6. Hand off events, offices and quotes as listed above.
7. Write the narrowest accurate correction.
