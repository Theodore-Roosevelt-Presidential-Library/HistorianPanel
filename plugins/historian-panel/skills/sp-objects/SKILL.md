# Objects and artifacts

Read `reference/panel-rules.md` at the plugin root first.

## Scope

- Specific artifacts associated with T.R. and his family: firearms, knives,
  swords, saddles and tack, ranch gear, uniforms and clothing, furniture,
  books from his library, medals, trophies and personal effects.
- Where an object is held now, and whether it is owned, on loan, on long-term
  deposit, or a reproduction.
- Provenance: the documented chain of ownership from T.R. to the present.
- Whether an object is an original, a period example of the same type, a
  replica, or a later copy.
- Credit lines and catalog numbers in Library copy.

## Out of scope

- Object types, prices and technology dates in general → `sp-material-culture`
- Images of objects and who appears with them → `sp-photo-id`
- Natural history specimens and trophies as scientific records →
  `sp-museum-specimens`
- The houses and cabins themselves → `sp-roosevelt-sites`
- Why an honor was awarded and when → `sp-honors-namesakes`
- Indigenous cultural items and their provenance → flag for the Library's human
  reviewers (panel rules section 10)

## Where to look first

1. **The holding institution's catalog record** for the object: accession
   number, credit line, provenance notes. This is the controlling source for
   where it is and what it is.
2. **National Park Service collections at Roosevelt sites:** Sagamore Hill
   (https://www.nps.gov/sahi/learn/historyculture/collections.htm), the
   Birthplace, and Theodore Roosevelt National Park; NPS catalog records and
   collection pages.
3. **Smithsonian collections search** (collections.si.edu): National Museum of
   American History and other units' object records.
4. **American Museum of Natural History, Theodore Roosevelt Memorial**
   (https://www.amnh.org/exhibitions/permanent/theodore-roosevelt-memorial):
   objects shown in the memorial halls and their lenders.
5. **Theodore Roosevelt Collection, Harvard Library**
   (https://library.harvard.edu/collections/theodore-roosevelt-collection):
   personal items as well as papers.
6. **Theodore Roosevelt Center digital library**
   (https://www.theodorerooseveltcenter.org/): correspondence about gifts,
   purchases and presentation pieces; its subject pages (for example the Medal
   of Honor page) gather related documents.
7. **White House Historical Association collections**
   (https://www.whitehousehistory.org/collections): objects in the Executive
   Mansion and their history.
8. **Firearm records:** maker records and factory letters, the NRA National
   Firearms Museum (https://www.nramuseum.org/), and auction catalogs with
   documented provenance (leads only).

## Known traps

- A replica, period example or "similar to" object is captioned as T.R.'s own.
  Check the catalog record's wording before saying "his." [method]
- An object on loan is described as owned by the displaying institution, or a
  loan is described as a gift. Read the credit line and say which it is.
  [method]
- Provenance rests on a family story or dealer's claim with no document.
  Say "attributed" or "said to be" unless a record links it to T.R. [method]
- Two similar objects (several rifles of one model, several saddles, more than
  one medal or copy of a medal) are treated as one. Use serial or accession
  numbers. [method]
- An object's location is given from an old article after it has moved.
  Confirm current location with the holder's record. [method]
- Dates of making, acquisition by T.R., and acquisition by the museum are
  mixed. Say which date the claim means. [method]
- Auction listings and collector sites are cited as provenance. They are leads
  until backed by a document. [method]
- The Library's own exhibit labels and object lists are not sources for an
  object's provenance. [house-rule]

## Procedure

1. Identify the object as precisely as possible (type, maker, serial or
   accession number, holder).
2. Find the current holder's catalog record and read the credit line.
3. Trace provenance back to T.R. through documents: letters, inventories,
   wills, deeds of gift, factory records.
4. Decide original, attributed, period example or reproduction, and owned,
   loaned or deposited.
5. Note any second holder claiming a similar object.
6. Write the narrowest accurate caption and credit line.
