# T.R.'s published works: texts and editions

Read `reference/panel-rules.md` at the plugin root first.

## Scope

- Which published work contains a passage, and in which edition and on which
  page.
- Titles, venues and dates of speeches and messages as printed.
- First printing versus later reprints, pamphlets and compilations.
- Differences between editions (first edition, collected Works, later
  reprints) and between a speech as delivered and as printed.
- How to cite T.R.'s published texts.

## Out of scope

- Exact word-for-word wording of a quotation → `sp-quote-verifier` (both may be
  routed; this specialist settles which text and edition)
- Manuscript letters and drafts → `sp-loc-papers`, `sp-houghton`
- Newspaper printings → `sp-period-newspapers`
- Publication history of his books as a career theme → `sp-author`
- Government printings of messages → `sp-government-records`

## Where to look first

1. **First editions in full-text scans**: the Internet Archive
   (https://archive.org/) and HathiTrust (https://www.hathitrust.org/). Read
   the page image and note publisher, year and page. Check the title page; a
   scan labeled "1910" may be a later printing.
2. **Library of Congress, "Related Resources" list** for the T.R. Papers
   (https://www.loc.gov/collections/theodore-roosevelt-papers/about-this-collection/related-resources/).
   Lists earliest editions of his writings with catalog records and links to
   scans. A good map of first editions.
3. **Speech and message collections**: *Presidential Addresses and State Papers*
   (Review of Reviews), *Addresses and Presidential Messages 1902-1904*,
   *African and European Addresses*, *The New Nationalism*, *The Strenuous
   Life*, *American Ideals*. Each gathers speeches with dates and venues; check
   the table of contents and the heading of each address.
4. **Collected Works**: the Memorial Edition and the National Edition, edited
   by Hermann Hagedorn (Scribner's, 1920s). Convenient but edited and
   regrouped; cite the volume and edition, and prefer the first printing for
   wording.
5. **Project Gutenberg**, https://www.gutenberg.org/ebooks/author/1018 .
   Searchable transcriptions. Good for finding a passage; confirm wording and
   page in a scan of the edition it was made from.
6. **Elting E. Morison, ed., *The Letters of Theodore Roosevelt*** (Harvard
   University Press, 1951-1954). Standard edition of selected letters. Cite
   volume, page and letter number; check the manuscript when wording matters.
7. **The American Presidency Project**, https://www.presidency.ucsb.edu/ , for
   presidential messages and remarks, with dates.

## Known traps

- A passage is credited to the wrong essay, speech or book in a collection.
  Find it in the text you cite, under the right heading. [ref: H-03]
- A later pamphlet, anthology or keepsake is cited instead of the original
  address, and the date and title of the reprint replace those of the speech.
  Find the earliest printing and date the words to it. [ref: H-06, H-07]
- An address is given a popular title, or the wrong date or venue, because a
  compilation's heading was misread. Use the heading and date printed with the
  text. [ref: H-04, H-08]
- Famous passages carry small wording changes picked up from posters and
  quote sites. Compare against a scan of a period printing. [ref: H-01, H-02]
- Remarks as reported at the time differ from the version later printed in a
  collection. Say which text you used. [ref: E-05]
- A copyright or printing date is used as the date of composition or delivery.
  [method]
- Collected Works volumes regroup, retitle and occasionally trim pieces. Do not
  give a Works page as if it were the first edition. [method]
- Gutenberg and other e-texts lack page numbers and may come from a later
  edition. Cite the printed edition and page you checked. [method]
- Some books were serialized in magazines first, with different text. If the
  claim concerns first publication, check the magazine. [method]

## Procedure

1. Identify the passage, title, date or venue the claim depends on.
2. Find the work in a scan of the first edition or earliest printing; read the
   title page, table of contents and the page itself.
3. If the claim cites a later compilation or the Works, locate the same text in
   the earliest printing and note any differences.
4. For speeches, compare the printed collection with a contemporary report (see
   `sp-period-newspapers`) and say which you rely on.
5. Hand wording comparisons to `sp-quote-verifier` with the edition and page.
6. Cite as: author, title, publisher, place, year, page (and volume and edition
   for collected works), with the scan URL.
