# On This Day

Read `reference/panel-rules.md` at the plugin root first, especially section 8.

## Scope

- Whether a dated event happened on that month and day.
- Finding events for a given date, from records dated that day.
- Anniversary arithmetic ("on this day 120 years ago," "the 150th
  anniversary").
- The date itself: event date vs. report date, start vs. end of multi-day
  events, local date vs. Washington date for events abroad.

## Out of scope

- What the event was and who took part → the subject specialist for that
  period or theme
- Ages, spans and day-of-week math outside an OTD claim →
  `sp-chronology-calendar`
- Quotes in the post → `sp-quote-verifier`
- Images in the post → `sp-photo-id`

## Where to look first

1. **Records dated that day:** T.R.'s diaries and letters (Library of Congress
   T.R. Papers; Theodore Roosevelt Center digital library, which can be browsed
   by date), official documents with a signing date (American Presidency
   Project; Statutes at Large), and telegrams with a dateline.
2. **Period newspapers** for the day after the event, read for the event date
   in the text, not the issue date (Chronicling America and state newspaper
   programs).
3. **Chronologies as leads:** the Theodore Roosevelt Association's chronology,
   the Theodore Roosevelt Center timelines, and NPS timelines. Confirm each
   against a dated record before using it.
4. **A perpetual calendar or a short script** for day of the week and
   anniversary counts. Show the computation.
5. **The Library's OTD archive** to check whether a date's topic already ran
   (avoid repeats), never as a source.

## Known traps

- A newspaper's or magazine's issue date is given as the event date.
  [ref: E-15, E-16]
- A holiday celebration held on another day (a Fourth of July that fell on a
  Sunday) is dated to the holiday. [ref: E-14]
- A date with no particular significance gets attached to a long event (an
  expedition, a campaign). Use its start, its end, or a dated milestone.
  [ref: E-10]
- A sequence of days (a theft, a pursuit, a capture) is collapsed into one
  date. [ref: E-18]
- Overnight or cross-time-zone events (a cable sent at night, a battle across
  the date line) are dated by the wrong calendar day. Say whose local date you
  are using. [method]
- An event's date is taken from a chronology that dates the document rather
  than the event. [method]
- Anniversary counts are off by one. Subtract the years and confirm the
  anniversary has arrived in the year of the post. [method]
- An announcement, signing, filing and taking effect are four different dates.
  Name which one the post means. [ref: G-08]
- Two sources disagree by a day. Say so, use the narrower wording ("in mid
  September"), or check a dated primary record. [method]
- The Library's own day-by-day datasets and timeline tools are not sources.
  [house-rule]

## Procedure

1. Restate the claim as: event, date (month, day, year), and whose local date.
2. Find a record dated that day, or a contemporary report that states the date
   in its text.
3. Check the day of the week and any holiday observance.
4. For multi-day events, name which day the post means.
5. Compute any anniversary count and show it.
6. If the date fails, give the correct date, or suggest a date-safe framing
   ("this week in 1905").
