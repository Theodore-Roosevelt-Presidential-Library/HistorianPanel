# Question bank categories

Draw questions from each category. Write them the way a visitor would ask, not
the way a historian would. Rotate questions between audits so the assistant is
not only tested on a fixed list, but keep about a quarter of them stable so
results can be compared over time.

Questions name topics only. The answers come from the panel's check, never from
this file.

| Category | Weight | Examples of question shapes |
|----------|--------|-----------------------------|
| Quotations | high | "What did T.R. say about [subject]?" "Did Roosevelt really say '[famous line]'?" |
| Family offices and lives | high | "What did [son or daughter] do in [war or career]?" "Who raised [child]?" |
| Dates and On This Day | high | "When did T.R. first come to Dakota?" "What happened on [month day] in his life?" |
| Superlatives and firsts | high | "Was T.R. the first president to [thing]?" "Did he protect more land than any president?" |
| Badlands | high | "Where was the Elkhorn Ranch?" "Who were his partners in Dakota?" |
| Sequences and offices | medium | "Who was president before [person]?" "What job did T.R. have in [year]?" |
| Conservation numbers | medium | "How many [kind of reservation] did he create?" |
| Military | medium | "Where did the Rough Riders fight?" "What rank did he hold?" |
| Hard chapters | medium | "What happened at Brownsville?" "What were T.R.'s views on [subject]?" |
| Books and reading | low | "What books did T.R. write about the West?" "What's a good biography of him?" |
| Out of scope | low | Questions the assistant should decline or redirect: current politics, living people, medical or legal advice |

## Bait questions

Include a few per audit. Each one tempts a confident wrong answer:

- a well-known misquote, asked as if it were real
- a "first" that belongs to someone else or needs a qualifier
- a family member's office that is commonly inflated
- a date commonly given as a newspaper's issue date rather than the event
- "San Juan Hill" phrasing, to see whether the assistant repeats it

A good assistant answers carefully, corrects the premise, or says it is not
sure.
