# Governor and Vice President

Read `reference/panel-rules.md` at the plugin root first.

## Scope

- The 1898 New York governor's race and the transition from the Rough Riders.
- His term as governor: legislation, appointments, the franchise tax fight,
  relations with Thomas C. Platt and the state Republican organization.
- How he came to be nominated for Vice President: the 1900 Republican National
  Convention, the roles of Platt, Mark Hanna and others.
- The 1900 campaign and its results.
- The vice presidency: presiding over the Senate, speeches, travel.
- The McKinley assassination: the shooting, the vigil, the Adirondack trip,
  the ride to the railhead, the oath of office and where it was taken.

## Out of scope

- Quotations, including the famous lines attributed to Hanna and Platt →
  `sp-quote-verifier` (also routed here for the context)
- Who held an office before or after him, and term lengths →
  `sp-officeholder-sequences`
- Age at the oath, "youngest" and other firsts → `sp-chronology-calendar` for
  the arithmetic, `sp-superlatives` for the ranking
- Photographs of the oath, its setting or the funeral → `sp-photo-id`
- The presidency from the oath onward → `sp-first-term`

## Where to look first

1. **T.R.'s own record:** *An Autobiography* (1913; Project Gutenberg,
   https://www.gutenberg.org/ebooks/3335), his gubernatorial messages and
   public papers, and his letters in the Library of Congress Theodore Roosevelt
   Papers (https://www.loc.gov/collections/theodore-roosevelt-papers/). The
   autobiography is retrospective; check dates against the letters.
2. **Theodore Roosevelt Center digital library**
   (https://www.theodorerooseveltcenter.org/digital-library/): scans of letters
   and clippings, searchable by date. Read the scan, not only the summary.
3. **National Park Service, Theodore Roosevelt Inaugural National Historic
   Site** (https://www.nps.gov/thri/index.htm), including its day-by-day page
   on the oath (https://www.nps.gov/thri/september14.htm). Good for the Buffalo
   sequence; check its sources for times of day.
4. **U.S. Senate Historical Office, Vice President biography**
   (https://www.senate.gov/about/officers-staff/vice-president/VP_Theodore_Roosevelt.htm):
   the official account of the vice presidency and its length.
5. **Election returns:** National Archives Electoral College results
   (https://www.archives.gov/electoral-college/1900) and the American
   Presidency Project (https://www.presidency.ucsb.edu/statistics/elections/1900).
6. **Period newspapers** in original page images through Chronicling America
   (https://chroniclingamerica.loc.gov/). Convention and Buffalo coverage was
   written fast and corrected later; compare several days.
7. **New York State Archives** (https://www.archives.nysed.gov/) for governor's
   office records and state legislative documents.
8. **Secondary, for leads only:** G. Wallace Chessman, *Governor Theodore
   Roosevelt: The Albany Apprenticeship, 1898-1900* (1965); Edmund Morris, *The
   Rise of Theodore Roosevelt* (1979) and *Theodore Rex* (2001). Follow their
   notes to the primary record.

## Known traps

- Barbed lines credited to Hanna or other party leaders about T.R. and the
  presidency survive mainly in later memoirs. They are presented as things said
  on the record, or moved to a different moment (the convention, the
  assassination, the funeral train). Label them as recalled, by whom, where.
  [ref: F-07, F-11, D-18]
- A memorable line is credited to a letter on a named date that no one can
  produce. Ask for the letter itself before using the citation. [ref: F-08]
- The night trip from the Adirondacks is retold with details (vehicles,
  relays, who woke him, at what hour) that differ between accounts written at
  the time and decades later. Prefer the contemporary record and give the
  narrowest wording. [method]
- The days between the shooting and McKinley's death are compressed: when T.R.
  left Buffalo, when he was recalled, when he arrived and where he went first
  are often in the wrong order. Build a dated timeline before checking any
  single step. [method]
- "Platt pushed him out of Albany" is stated as the only motive for the 1900
  nomination. Accounts by participants disagree about who wanted what; say so
  rather than pick one. [method]
- The length of his governorship or vice presidency is described loosely
  ("barely a year," "only months"). Compute it from the oath and departure
  dates. [method]
- Results of the 1900 election are described with words like "landslide" or
  "substantial majority" without the returns. Check the official counts and use
  measured wording. [method]
- The Library's own timelines and day-by-day tools are not independent
  sources. [house-rule]

## Procedure

1. Pin the claim to a date or date range and a place (Albany, Philadelphia,
   Oyster Bay, Buffalo, the Adirondacks, Washington).
2. Build a short dated timeline from contemporary sources for the days around
   the claim before judging any one detail.
3. Look first for T.R.'s own contemporary record (letter, message, diary), then
   official records (Senate, Electoral College, state papers), then a period
   newspaper read in its page image.
4. For anything said by a third party, find the earliest printing and note
   whether it is a memoir recollection.
5. Compute any length of service or interval and show the arithmetic.
6. Route quotations, sequences of officeholders and images to their
   specialists.
7. Write the narrowest accurate correction.
