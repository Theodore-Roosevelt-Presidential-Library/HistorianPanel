# Civil Service Commission and NYC Police Board

Read `reference/panel-rules.md` at the plugin root first.

## Scope

- His appointment to the U.S. Civil Service Commission, his fellow
  commissioners, and the presidents he served under.
- The Commission's investigations, reports, rules and the growth of the
  classified service while he served.
- His appointment to the New York City Board of Police Commissioners, his
  role as its president, and his fellow commissioners.
- Police board actions: enforcement of the excise laws, personnel and
  discipline reforms, the night inspections, the reporters who went with him,
  and the board's deadlocks.
- His leaving each post.

## Out of scope

- The Navy Department, from appointment on → `sp-navy-rough-riders`
- Who held a commission seat or office, in what order → 
  `sp-officeholder-sequences`
- Jacob Riis, Lincoln Steffens and other figures as subjects in themselves →
  flag; `sp-political-literary-circle` once added
- Quotes → `sp-quote-verifier` (also routed here)
- Photographs → `sp-photo-id`
- "First" and "only" claims about reforms → `sp-superlatives`

## Where to look first

1. **Annual Reports of the U.S. Civil Service Commission** for his years,
   printed as government documents. Read in scans at govinfo
   (https://www.govinfo.gov/) in the Serial Set, or at HathiTrust
   (https://www.hathitrust.org/). The reports carry the commissioners' names,
   dates of service and the official figures.
2. **National Archives, Records of the U.S. Civil Service Commission (Record
   Group 146)** (https://www.archives.gov/research/guide-fed-records/groups/146.html):
   what Commission records survive, including minutes and correspondence.
3. **NYC Municipal Archives** (https://www.archives.nyc/): city government
   records. Check what police board minutes and reports survive before citing
   them.
4. **T.R.'s letters and his own accounts:** Library of Congress, Theodore
   Roosevelt Papers (https://www.loc.gov/collections/theodore-roosevelt-papers/);
   Theodore Roosevelt Center scans
   (https://www.theodorerooseveltcenter.org/digital-library/); *An
   Autobiography* (1913), Project Gutenberg #3335
   (https://www.gutenberg.org/ebooks/3335), which has separate chapters for the
   two posts.
5. **Period newspapers, read on the page:** Chronicling America
   (https://www.loc.gov/collections/chronicling-america/) and NYS Historic
   Newspapers (https://nyshistoricnewspapers.org/). The New York press was
   partisan and some reporters were his friends; weigh accordingly.
6. **Memoirs by reporters who covered him,** such as Jacob Riis, *The Making
   of an American* (1901). Recollection, not record; label it.
7. **Secondary, for leads only:** Richard Zacks, *Island of Vice* (2012);
   Edmund Morris, *The Rise of Theodore Roosevelt* (1979).

## Known traps

- A passage he wrote about one post is quoted as if it described the other.
  Find the line in the chapter you cite and match the chapter to the job.
  [ref: F-27]
- The years between ranch life and the police board are compressed, dropping
  the Commission years. Give each post its own dates. [ref: D-19]
- Commission figures (positions classified, examinations, removals) are
  credited to T.R. personally or to the wrong year. Use the annual report for
  that year and say what it counts. [method]
- "Police commissioner" and "president of the board" are used as one title,
  and the board is described as if he alone ran it. Confirm titles and votes.
  [method]
- Night-patrol stories survive mostly in reporters' memoirs and grow in the
  retelling. Date them from contemporary newspaper reports where possible, and
  label the rest as recalled. [method]
- Newspaper issue dates are used as the dates of board actions. Find the
  meeting date. [method]
- Credit for a reform is given to him when it came from a statute, the full
  board or a predecessor. Check the enabling law and the board record.
  [method]
- The Library's own site and tools are not sources. [house-rule]

## Procedure

1. Decide which post the claim is about, and pin it to a date.
2. For Commission claims, find the annual report for that year; for police
   claims, the board record or a contemporary newspaper read on the page.
3. Check T.R.'s own letters and the matching chapter of his autobiography.
4. Compute any span, count or tenure from dated sources and show it.
5. Label memoir material as recalled, naming who recalled it.
6. When sources disagree, give the narrowest wording all good sources support
   and say so in `notes`.
7. Write the narrowest accurate correction.
