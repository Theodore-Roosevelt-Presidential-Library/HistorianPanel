# Superlatives, firsts and absolutes

Read `reference/panel-rules.md` at the plugin root first.

## Scope

"First," "only," "last," "youngest," "oldest," "largest," "most," "more than
any," "record," "never," "always," "unprecedented," "coined," "invented," and
"the most quoted," wherever they appear about T.R., his family or his era.

## Out of scope

The underlying number or date when there is no ranking (route to the
specialist for that subject). This specialist is always added alongside the
subject specialist.

## Where to look first

1. **A source that makes the same superlative with the same scope.** The claim
   stands only if a reliable source says the same thing, about the same
   category, as of the same time.
2. **The subject's authoritative sources** (see the subject specialist's list)
   to confirm the facts under the superlative.
3. **Counterexample searches:** look for earlier or larger cases. Search the
   category directly ("first president to ride in an automobile," "presidents
   who declined a second term") and check the predecessors.
4. **Dictionaries of record** (the Oxford English Dictionary, cited through a
   reliable intermediary if needed) for "coined" and "first used" claims.

## Known traps

- "First" claims hide a qualifier: first in public, first while in office, first
  as a former president. Find the qualifier and keep it. [ref: E-09, D-11, D-12]
- "More than any president before or since" fails when a later president
  surpassed the figure. Rank only what a source ranks, as of its date.
  [ref: D-13, D-14]
- Unmeasurable superlatives ("the most quoted passage," "the most famous") have
  no source. Narrow to "one of" or cut. [ref: D-07]
- "Coined" is claimed for a term that existed earlier; "popularized" is usually
  what the sources support. [ref: D-15]
- "The only one ever" repeats from source to source with no underlying record.
  Find the record or hedge to "often described as." [ref: A-19]
- "No one had ever done this" has earlier counterexamples. Search for them before
  accepting it. [ref: G-06, B-04]
- "Youngest president" has two different meanings: youngest to take office and
  youngest elected. Say which. [method]

## Procedure

1. Name the category, the measure, and the time frame the superlative claims.
2. Look for a source that states the same superlative. If none, the claim cannot
   stand as written.
3. Search for counterexamples, earliest and latest.
4. If the superlative fails, write the narrowest true version: add the
   qualifier, change "the most" to "one of the most," or state the plain fact
   without ranking it.
