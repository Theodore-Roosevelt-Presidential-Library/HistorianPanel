# The Badlands years

Read `reference/panel-rules.md` at the plugin root first.

## Scope

- T.R.'s trips to and time in Dakota Territory and nearby Montana Territory,
  1883 onward, and his later visits and train stops.
- The Maltese Cross and Elkhorn ranches, the cattle business, and its end.
- Medora, the settlement of Little Missouri, Dickinson, Mingusville and the
  stockmen's association.
- His partners, foremen and neighbors.

## Out of scope

- Indigenous history of the region → flag for the Library's human reviewers
  (panel rules section 10)
- Geology, landforms and wildlife facts not tied to T.R. → flag; no specialist
  yet
- Quotes → `sp-quote-verifier` (also routed here)

## Where to look first

1. **T.R.'s own writing:** *Hunting Trips of a Ranchman*, *Ranch Life and the
   Hunting-Trail*, *An Autobiography*, and his letters (Library of Congress
   T.R. Papers; Theodore Roosevelt Center scans).
2. **His diaries and journals** for the period (Library of Congress; Theodore
   Roosevelt Center scans). Day-by-day entries settle dates.
3. **National Park Service, Theodore Roosevelt National Park:** history pages and
   historic resource studies.
4. **Theodore Roosevelt Center, "Roosevelt in the Badlands" timeline** and
   encyclopedia, which cite primary records.
5. **Period newspapers** read in their original pages: the *Bad Lands Cow Boy*,
   the *Dickinson Press*, the *Bismarck Tribune* and others, through the holding
   institution's scans. Not through any dataset the Library built from them.
6. **State Historical Society of North Dakota** collections.
7. **Hermann Hagedorn, *Roosevelt in the Bad Lands* (1921):** valuable, and it
   reprints period documents, but much of it rests on reminiscences gathered
   decades later. Prefer the documents it reprints over its narrative. [method]

## Known traps

- A newspaper's issue date is used as the date of the event it reports.
  [ref: E-15, E-16]
- Holidays that fell on a Sunday were often observed on Monday. Check the day of
  the week. [ref: E-14]
- The 1883 hunting trip and the 1884 return after the deaths of his wife and
  mother are merged into one "grief-stricken" arrival. Ages differ between the
  two trips. [ref: D-23]
- Events are placed in Dakota that happened across the line in Montana
  Territory. [ref: D-25]
- The end of the Elkhorn is dated differently by different sources (foremen
  leaving, the house kept open, the ranch sold). Say which milestone you mean.
  [ref: E-20, E-15]
- Day-by-day sequences (a theft, a pursuit, a capture) are compressed into one
  date. Use the journal entries. [ref: E-18]
- Story Guide dates and places are leads only and have contained errors.
  [ref: audit-2026-09-07]
- North Dakota landforms are buttes, not mesas. [house-rule]
- The Library's own Badlands tools and datasets are not independent sources.
  [house-rule]

## Procedure

1. Pin the claim to a date or date range and a place.
2. Look for T.R.'s own contemporary record first (diary, letter, his books), then
   a period newspaper read in the original, then NPS or the Theodore Roosevelt
   Center.
3. Check the day of the week for any date.
4. When later reminiscence and a contemporary record disagree, prefer the record
   and say so.
5. Write the narrowest accurate correction.
