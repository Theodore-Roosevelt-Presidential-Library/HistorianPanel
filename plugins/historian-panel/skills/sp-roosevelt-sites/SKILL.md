# Roosevelt sites

Read `reference/panel-rules.md` at the plugin root first.

## Scope

- The physical history of places tied to T.R.: built, altered, demolished,
  rebuilt, moved, restored.
- The birthplace townhouse and its reconstruction; Sagamore Hill; the Maltese
  Cross cabin and the Elkhorn ranch site; the Wilcox House in Buffalo; Theodore
  Roosevelt Island; Pine Knot; and other homes and lodgings.
- Designations and ownership: national historic site, national memorial,
  National Register and Landmark status; which agency or group owns and runs it.
- The history of the land where the Library now stands. Claims about the
  Library as an institution are out of scope except as history.

## Out of scope

- What T.R. did at a Badlands site → `sp-badlands`
- Place names, boundaries and distances → `sp-maps-places`
- Objects inside the houses → `sp-objects`
- Memorials and statues → `sp-legacy-commemoration`
- Parks and features named for him (other than the sites listed) →
  `sp-honors-namesakes`
- The Library's current programs, visitation and operations → not a panel
  question; refer to the Library's staff
- Indigenous history of a site → flag for the Library's human reviewers
  (panel rules section 10)

## Where to look first

1. **National Park Service unit history pages:** Birthplace
   (https://www.nps.gov/thrb/learn/historyculture/index.htm), Sagamore Hill
   (https://www.nps.gov/sahi/learn/historyculture/index.htm), Inaugural Site
   (https://www.nps.gov/thri/learn/historyculture/index.htm), Theodore Roosevelt
   Island (https://www.nps.gov/this/learn/historyculture/index.htm), Theodore
   Roosevelt National Park (https://www.nps.gov/thro/learn/historyculture/index.htm),
   including the Maltese Cross cabin and Elkhorn Ranch pages.
2. **NPS historic structure reports, administrative histories and
   foundation documents** collected at NPS History (https://npshistory.com/):
   the detailed record of moves, reconstructions and restorations.
3. **National Register and National Historic Landmark nomination files**
   (NPGallery, https://npgallery.nps.gov/NRHP/): dates of construction and
   alteration, with sources.
4. **NPS, *The Presidents* historical survey** (for example
   https://www.nps.gov/parkhistory/online_books/presidents/site42.htm): short
   site histories; older, so check against newer reports.
5. **Site operators' own histories:** Theodore Roosevelt Inaugural Site
   Foundation (trsite.org), Pine Knot (https://www.pineknot.org/). Useful, but
   not independent of the site.
6. **Period newspapers and T.R.'s letters** (Chronicling America; Theodore
   Roosevelt Center, https://www.theodorerooseveltcenter.org/) for construction,
   moves and visits in his lifetime.
7. **Deeds, acts of Congress and proclamations** establishing each site.

## Known traps

- A reconstruction is described as the original building, or a rebuilt house
  as the room where events happened. Say "reconstructed" or "on the site of."
  [method]
- A cabin that was moved is described as standing where T.R. used it. Check
  the site's record of each move and where it stands now. [method]
- The date a house was built, the date the family bought it, the date T.R.
  lived there and the date it became a public site are mixed. Name which date.
  [method]
- Ownership and management are confused (owned by one body, run by another, or
  transferred over time). Use the current official record. [method]
- Rooms and furnishings are said to be original when they are restored or
  period pieces. Check the historic structure report or furnishing plan.
  [method]
- Site histories written by the operating group are counted as independent of
  each other or of NPS. Trace their sources. [method]
- The Library's own site histories, tours and campus materials are not sources.
  [house-rule]
- Landforms around the Badlands sites are buttes, never mesas. [house-rule]

## Procedure

1. Identify the site and the specific structure or feature the claim concerns.
2. Read the NPS or operator history page, then the historic structure report or
   National Register file it rests on.
3. Classify the structure: original in place, original moved, restored,
   reconstructed, or site only.
4. Separate the dates: built, acquired, occupied, altered, moved, designated,
   opened.
5. Confirm current ownership and management from the official record.
6. Write the narrowest accurate correction.
