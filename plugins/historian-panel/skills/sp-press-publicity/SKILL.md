# T.R. and the press

Read `reference/panel-rules.md` at the plugin root first.

## Scope

- T.R.'s relations with reporters and editors: access, favored correspondents,
  briefings, leaks, the press room in the executive offices.
- The "bully pulpit": where the phrase comes from and how it is used.
- The speech on the man with the muck-rake, every occasion on which he used
  the theme, and the history of the word muckraker.
- Journalists and muckrakers as figures in his story.
- His work as a journalist and editor, including his magazine and newspaper
  connections after the presidency.
- Image-making: posed photographs, publicity, campaign press operations.
- How a reported event reached print: wire services, special correspondents,
  eyewitnesses, rewrites.

## Out of scope

- Exact wording of the muck-rake speech or any quote → `sp-quote-verifier`
  (also routed here)
- "Coined," "first," "invented" a word or practice → `sp-superlatives`
  (this specialist finds the word history; that one rules on the superlative)
- Who and what appears in a photograph → `sp-photo-id`
- Film, recordings and cartoons → `sp-film-sound-cartoons`
- T.R.'s books and their editions → `sp-author`
- Reading period newspapers as sources for other topics → `sp-period-newspapers`

## Where to look first

1. **T.R.'s own texts** of the speeches and articles, read in a printing of the
   time: *Presidential Addresses and State Papers*, his *Outlook* and
   *Metropolitan* articles, on HathiTrust (hathitrust.org) or the Internet
   Archive (https://archive.org/). The American Presidency Project
   (https://www.presidency.ucsb.edu/) for presidential texts.
2. **His correspondence with journalists and editors** (Library of Congress
   Theodore Roosevelt Papers,
   https://www.loc.gov/collections/theodore-roosevelt-papers/; Theodore
   Roosevelt Center, https://www.theodorerooseveltcenter.org/).
3. **Period newspapers** in the original (Chronicling America,
   https://www.loc.gov/collections/chronicling-america/about-this-collection/): compare wire copy
   across several papers to tell a shared dispatch from independent reporting.
4. **Oxford English Dictionary** (https://www.oed.com/, subscription) for first
   recorded uses of words and phrases. Other dictionaries' etymology notes are
   leads.
5. **Journalists' memoirs and the muckrakers' own writing** (for example Lincoln
   Steffens, Ray Stannard Baker, Ida Tarbell): useful but recalled, often
   decades later; label as recalled.
6. **White House Historical Association**
   (https://www.whitehousehistory.org/): history of the press room and press
   arrangements.
7. **Standard secondary works** (secondary): George Juergens, *News from the
   White House* (1981); Doris Kearns Goodwin, *The Bully Pulpit* (2013).

## Known traps

- T.R. is credited with coining a word or phrase that predates him. Check the
  dictionary's earliest citations before saying he coined, rather than
  popularized, it. [ref: D-15]
- An anecdote is framed as an exchange with a reporter when it survives only in
  a friend's memoir. Name who recalled it and where. [ref: C-14]
- One wire dispatch printed in many papers is counted as many independent
  reports. Identify the shared text and count it once. [method]
- A newspaper's version of a speech is treated as the delivered text, or the
  later book text as what reporters heard. Say which version you used. [method]
- Private remarks and a later public speech on the same theme are merged into
  one event. Date and place each occasion. [method]
- Posed or staged photographs are described as candid, or a publicity
  arrangement as spontaneous. Look for the photographer's or agency's record
  and contemporary accounts. [method]
- A newspaper's issue date is used as the date of the event it reports. Find
  the dateline. [method]
- Write T.R., never "Teddy," except in quoted headlines or titles. [house-rule]

## Procedure

1. State the claim: what was said, written, staged or reported, by whom, when.
2. Find T.R.'s own text or letter of the time.
3. For a report, find the earliest printing, its dateline, and whether it is
   wire copy or a correspondent's own account.
4. For word or phrase origins, check the dictionary's earliest citations and
   pass any coined or first wording to `sp-superlatives`.
5. Label memoir material as recalled, with who, where and when.
6. Write the narrowest accurate correction.
