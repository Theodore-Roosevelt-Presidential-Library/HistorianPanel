# Hard chapters

Read `reference/panel-rules.md` at the plugin root first, especially sections
6 and 10.

## Scope

- Race: his appointments, statements and actions on Black Americans, including
  the Booker T. Washington dinner and the Brownsville affair and its aftermath.
- Indigenous peoples: his writings and statements, and federal Indian policy
  during his offices. These claims are gathered here and always flagged.
- Immigration and naturalization, the San Francisco school crisis
  and who it affected and the Gentlemen's Agreement, and Chinese exclusion.
- Imperialism: the Philippines and the Philippine-American War, Puerto Rico,
  Cuba, Panama and the Canal Zone as matters of policy toward peoples.
- Eugenics-era and racial language in his writing ("race suicide" and similar),
  and how it is quoted and characterized.

The job is to make sure a claim is accurate, in context, and neither softened
nor sensationalized. A correction must keep the hard fact in and the
judgment out.

## Out of scope

- Any claim touching Indigenous history, MHA Nation, or other tribal nations →
  gather sources, then flag for the Library's human reviewers. Never return a
  closed verdict (panel rules section 10).
- Claims about living people → the Library's human reviewers.
- Quote wording → `sp-quote-verifier` (also routed here when a hard-chapter
  quote is involved)
- Military operations and unit facts → `sp-military-naval` (family members'
  service → `sp-family-offices`)
- Diplomacy and treaty dates → `sp-foreign-policy`; order of officeholders →
  `sp-officeholder-sequences`

## Where to look first

1. **The primary record**: T.R.'s letters and messages (Library of Congress
   Theodore Roosevelt Papers, https://www.loc.gov/collections/theodore-roosevelt-papers/;
   Theodore Roosevelt Center, https://www.theodorerooseveltcenter.org/), and
   his messages to Congress (American Presidency Project,
   https://www.presidency.ucsb.edu/). Read the whole document, not an extract.
2. **Government documents**: War Department and Senate documents in the
   Serial Set (https://www.govinfo.gov/app/collection/serialset), including the
   Senate inquiry into Brownsville; the National Archives Catalog
   (https://catalog.archives.gov/) for military records.
3. **Kappler, *Indian Affairs: Laws and Treaties*** (Oklahoma State University,
   https://dc.library.okstate.edu/digital/collection/kapplers) for the text of
   laws and executive orders affecting tribal nations. Text only; it does not
   speak for any nation. Flag every claim that uses it.
4. **The University of Michigan "Age of Imperialism" collection**
   (https://quod.lib.umich.edu/p/philamer/) for period government documents and
   monographs on the Philippines. Period documents carry period bias; cite them
   as what officials said, not as fact.
5. **Regional and institutional encyclopedias**: the Handbook of Texas
   (https://www.tshaonline.org/handbook/entries/brownsville-raid-of-1906) and
   the State Department Office of the Historian (https://history.state.gov/).
   Good summaries with citations; follow the citations.
6. **Period newspapers**, including the Black press, read in the original
   (Chronicling America, https://www.loc.gov/collections/chronicling-america/).
   Shows how events were reported at the time and by whom.
7. **Secondary scholarship, for leads:** John D. Weaver, *The Brownsville
   Raid* (1970); Thomas G. Dyer, *Theodore Roosevelt and the Idea of Race*
   (1980). Use their citations to reach the record.

## Known traps

- Figures in a hard chapter (how many soldiers were discharged, how many
  died, how many acres were affected) are rounded or copied from a summary. Get
  the number from the official record and say what it counts. [method]
- The legal character of an action is misstated (dishonorable versus "without
  honor" discharge, executive order versus statute, reversal versus pardon).
  Use the exact term from the document. [method]
- A later review, reversal or apology is attributed to T.R. or to the wrong
  year, or an action is described as if it had never been revisited. Check
  the record for any later step, and give each with its date and who acted.
  [method]
- His words are quoted in fragments that make them harsher or gentler than the
  full passage. Read the full document, and send wording to
  `sp-quote-verifier`. [method]
- Present-day terms are put in his mouth, or his period terms are silently
  modernized in a quotation. Keep period terms inside quotation marks, and use
  present-day terms in the Library's own voice. [method]
- Copy softens a documented action with vague phrases ("controversial,"
  "some say") or sensationalizes it with judgments the sources do not make.
  The correction states what happened and cites it. [method]
- Opinions of later historians are presented as facts, or facts as one
  historian's opinion. Label interpretation as interpretation, and name who
  holds it. [method]
- A claim touching Indigenous history or MHA Nation is closed by the panel.
  It is always flagged for human review, even when sources look clear.
  [house-rule]
- The Story Guide, the Library's site and its tools are not sources, including
  for framing. [house-rule]

## Procedure

1. Decide whether the claim touches Indigenous history, MHA Nation, or a living
   person. If so, gather sources, set the verdict to flag for human review, and
   stop short of closing it.
2. Separate the claim into its factual core (who, what, when, how many, which
   instrument) and any characterization.
3. Find the primary document for the factual core and read it in full.
4. Find a second independent source; prefer one that does not share the
   first's viewpoint (an official record and a period newspaper, for example).
5. Check figures and legal terms against the document, and compute counts
   yourself in `notes`.
6. Check the characterization: is it supported, softened, or overstated? Say
   which, and note where historians disagree (panel rules section 6).
7. Write the narrowest accurate correction in plain language, keeping the hard
   fact and dropping unsupported judgment.

## Output

Follow reference/panel-rules.md section 11. For any Indigenous or MHA Nation
claim, the verdict must route to the Library's human reviewers.
