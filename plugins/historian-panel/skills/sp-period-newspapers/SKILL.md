# Period newspapers

Read `reference/panel-rules.md` at the plugin root first.

## Scope

- What a period newspaper or magazine printed, on what date and page.
- Dating events from press reports, including dispatch datelines.
- Speech texts and interviews as printed in the press.
- Wire and syndicated stories and how they spread.
- Contemporary reactions, nicknames and coverage claims.

## Out of scope

- Exact wording of a quotation for publication → `sp-quote-verifier` (this
  specialist finds the printing; both may be routed)
- Book versions of speeches and articles → `sp-published-works`
- Photographs and cartoons printed in papers → `sp-photo-id`, `sp-film-sound-cartoons`
- Badlands chronology → `sp-badlands` (also routed here for Dakota papers)

## Where to look first

1. **Chronicling America**, https://www.loc.gov/collections/chronicling-america/ .
   Free page images of digitized U.S. newspapers with OCR text. Coverage is
   uneven by state, title and year; absence here is not absence from the press.
2. **State digital newspaper programs**, for example NYS Historic Newspapers
   (https://nyshistoricnewspapers.org/), the California Digital Newspaper
   Collection (https://cdnc.ucr.edu/), and the State Historical Society of North
   Dakota (https://www.history.nd.gov/). Many titles not in Chronicling
   America.
3. **Licensed archives** (for example the historical New York Times, and
   commercial newspaper databases) where the Library or a reviewer has access.
   Verify and cite only; never paste their text into anything public.
4. **Magazines of record**: *The Outlook*, *Harper's Weekly*, *Collier's* and
   others, through HathiTrust (https://www.hathitrust.org/) and the Internet
   Archive (https://archive.org/). Separate articles signed by T.R. from
   reports about him.
5. **Later books that reprint newspaper items** (for example Hagedorn's
   *Roosevelt in the Bad Lands*, 1921). Secondary; use them to find the
   original issue, then read the original.

## Known traps

- A newspaper's issue date is used as the date of the event it reports. Read
  the dateline and the text ("yesterday," "on Saturday") and compute the event
  date. [ref: E-15, E-16]
- Weekly papers report events days late, so a weekly's date can trail the
  event by most of a week. Check publication frequency. [ref: E-14]
- A speech text as reported by a local paper differs from later book versions,
  and copy "tidies" the newspaper wording into modern prose. Keep the printed
  wording and say which printing you used. [ref: E-13]
- Lines that appear only in a later memoir are presented as if newspapers
  printed them at the time. Find the contemporary printing or label the line as
  recalled. [ref: D-18, F-07, F-11]
- A wire story printed in dozens of papers is one source, not dozens. Find the
  earliest printing and record the others as `derives_from`. [method]
- OCR text misreads names, numbers and dates. Never cite OCR text; read the
  page image and check every figure. [method]
- Papers took sides. A partisan paper's account of a speech or crowd may differ
  from a rival paper's. Note the paper's affiliation when it matters and seek a
  second paper. [method]
- Reprints in later books silently trim or correct the newspaper text. Compare
  with the original issue. [method]
- Newspaper reports of what T.R. "said" are often paraphrases put in quotation
  marks. Treat reported speech as reported. [method]

## Procedure

1. Identify the paper, date, page and passage the claim depends on, or the
   event and date range to search.
2. Search Chronicling America and the relevant state program; then licensed
   archives if available.
3. Open the page image. Read the dateline and surrounding text; compute the
   event date from the issue date if needed, and check the day of the week.
4. Look for an earlier printing (wire origin, the paper of the city where the
   event happened) and for a second independent paper.
5. Compare any reprint in a later book with the original issue.
6. Cite as: paper title, city, issue date, page, column if available, with the
   page-image URL. For licensed sources, cite without quoting at length.
