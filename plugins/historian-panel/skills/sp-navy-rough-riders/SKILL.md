# Navy Department and the Rough Riders, 1897-1898

Read `reference/panel-rules.md` at the plugin root first.

## Scope

- His appointment as Assistant Secretary of the Navy, his work there, his
  dealings with the Secretary, and orders and cables he sent.
- His resignation and commission in the First U.S. Volunteer Cavalry; Leonard
  Wood; recruiting, training, and the regiment's officers and men.
- The campaign in Cuba: the landing, Las Guasimas, Kettle Hill and the San
  Juan Heights, the trenches before Santiago, sickness, and the round-robin
  letter.
- The return to Montauk and the regiment's mustering out.
- His book *The Rough Riders*, its magazine serialization and editions, and
  later honors for the 1898 action.

## Out of scope

- The governorship and after → flag; `sp-governor-vp` once added
- Naval and military history not centered on T.R. (Manila Bay as a battle,
  fleet movements, strategy) → flag; `sp-military-naval` once added
- Who held Navy or War Department offices, in order → 
  `sp-officeholder-sequences`
- Quotes, including cables and orders → `sp-quote-verifier` (also routed here)
- Photographs, paintings and film of the war → `sp-photo-id`
- Indigenous members of the regiment as subjects in themselves → flag for the
  Library's human reviewers
- "First," "only" and "most" claims → `sp-superlatives`

## Where to look first

1. **T.R., *The Rough Riders* (1899),** Project Gutenberg #13000
   (https://www.gutenberg.org/ebooks/13000), and *An Autobiography* (1913),
   #3335 (https://www.gutenberg.org/ebooks/3335). His own accounts; check
   which edition and whether the magazine version differs.
2. **Naval History and Heritage Command** (https://www.history.navy.mil/):
   documents on the Navy Department in 1897-1898, including cables and orders.
   Best for who sent what, and when.
3. **National Archives, Spanish-American War records**
   (https://www.archives.gov/research/military/spanish-american-war), including
   the regiment's casualty list for July 1-3, 1898, on DocsTeach
   (https://docsteach.org/document/casualty-list-rough-riders-july-1-to-3-1898-attachment-to-report-of-operations/).
   Official reports, rosters and service records.
4. **U.S. Army Center of Military History** (https://history.army.mil/):
   Santiago campaign histories and unit organization. Good for which units
   fought where.
5. **National Park Service:** Theodore Roosevelt Birthplace, "T. R. the Rough
   Rider" (https://www.nps.gov/thrb/learn/historyculture/tr-rr-spanamwar.htm),
   and "Buffalo Soldiers in the Spanish-American War"
   (https://www.nps.gov/articles/000/busospanamwar.htm). Useful overviews;
   check their figures against the reports.
6. **Period newspapers, read on the page:** the Library of Congress guide
   "Theodore Roosevelt's Rough Riders: Topics in Chronicling America"
   (https://guides.loc.gov/chronicling-america-theodore-roosevelt-rough-riders).
   War correspondents' dispatches are vivid but often wrong on details.
7. **Secondary, for leads only:** Clay Risen, *The Crowded Hour* (2019);
   Edmund Morris, *The Rise of Theodore Roosevelt* (1979). Confirm titles,
   subtitles and publishers in a library catalog before listing a book.

## Known traps

- Write "Kettle Hill" or "the San Juan Heights," never "San Juan Hill," in
  prose, alt text, link text and headings. Quote an official citation or a
  book title exactly, in quotation marks, if it uses the older name.
  [house-rule]
- Kettle Hill and the main San Juan ridge are merged into one charge, or the
  regiment is put on the wrong hill. Separate the two in T.R.'s own account
  and the Army history. [ref: E-12, F-14, D-22]
- Casualty figures confuse killed with killed-and-wounded, or one battle with
  the campaign. Use the casualty list and say exactly what it counts.
  [ref: E-01]
- The units that fought alongside the regiment are named wrongly, given the
  wrong branch, or left out. Check the order of battle.
  [ref: E-02]
- Intervals are miscounted (landing to first fight, days in the trenches).
  Compute them from dated reports. [ref: F-34]
- Battle narratives are dramatized past what T.R. and the reports say, for
  example about who gave an order or what tactic was used. Follow his account
  and the official reports. [ref: F-37]
- Separate orders from different officials and dates are merged into one
  dramatic order. Date each message and say who sent it. [ref: D-17]
- Cables and orders are "corrected" into smooth English. Keep the original
  wording, including telegraphic omissions. [ref: F-15]
- The regiment's official name is misspelled or garbled. Copy it from an
  official record. [ref: E-03]
- Reading lists carry the wrong year, subtitle, co-author or ISBN. Confirm
  every book in a library catalog. [ref: F-21, F-29, F-30]

## Procedure

1. Pin the claim to a date, a place and a unit or office.
2. For Navy Department claims, find the document at NHHC or the National
   Archives; for the war, T.R.'s own account plus an official report or roster.
3. Check every hill name against the house rule, and every casualty figure
   against the casualty list.
4. Compute intervals and check days of the week from dated sources.
5. When a correspondent's dispatch or later memoir disagrees with an official
   report, prefer the report and say so in `notes`.
6. Route quotes, images and offices-in-order to their specialists.
7. Write the narrowest accurate correction.
