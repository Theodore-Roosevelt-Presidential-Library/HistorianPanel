# Military and naval history

Read `reference/panel-rules.md` at the plugin root first.

## Scope

- *The Naval War of 1812* (1882) as a book and its reception in the Navy.
- T.R.'s National Guard service and its ranks.
- The Navy Department, 1897-1898: his duties, orders, cables and
  appropriations as Assistant Secretary.
- The Spanish-American War: the regiment's formation, officers, units,
  ranks, the voyage and landing, Las Guasimas, the San Juan Heights and
  Kettle Hill, the siege, casualties, Camp Wikoff and muster-out.
- Other units in the same actions, including the regular cavalry and
  infantry regiments and the Gatling gun detachment, as they bear on a claim.
- Naval policy as president: battleship building, the Great White Fleet and
  its route, dates and ports.
- Any 1917 proposal to raise a volunteer division, and what became of it.
- His Medal of Honor: any campaign for it, the award and its date.

## Out of scope

- Military service of family members, including Ted Jr., Kermit, Archie and
  Quentin → `sp-family-offices`
- Who held a cabinet or Navy office before or after whom →
  `sp-officeholder-sequences`
- Diplomacy around wars and the fleet (Portsmouth, the Gentlemen's Agreement,
  Panama) → `sp-foreign-policy`
- Quotations, including cables quoted verbatim → `sp-quote-verifier` (also
  routed here for the date and recipient)
- Photographs of battlefields and troops → `sp-photo-id`
- "First" and "only" claims → `sp-superlatives`

## Where to look first

1. **Official military records.** National Archives military records
   (archives.gov/research/military), including muster rolls, casualty lists
   and reports of operations (some on DocsTeach, docsteach.org). The
   authority for units, ranks, names and casualties. Expect several lists
   compiled on different dates.
2. **Naval History and Heritage Command (history.navy.mil).** Lists of
   Navy secretaries and assistant secretaries, the *Dictionary of American
   Naval Fighting Ships*, selected Spanish-American War documents and the
   Great White Fleet itinerary. Authoritative for naval dates, ship names and
   routes.
3. **T.R.'s own books.** *The Rough Riders* (1899, Project Gutenberg
   gutenberg.org/ebooks/13000), *The Naval War of 1812* (gutenberg.org/ebooks/
   9104) and *An Autobiography* (gutenberg.org/ebooks/3335). Primary for his
   account; check his numbers against the official rolls.
4. **Annual Reports of the Secretary of the Navy and of the War Department,**
   and the U.S. Serial Set (govinfo.gov/app/collection/serialset). Official
   reports of operations, appropriations and ship strength.
5. **U.S. Army Center of Military History (history.army.mil)** and the
   Congressional Medal of Honor Society (cmohs.org). Medal of Honor records,
   citations and dates of award, and Army unit lineage.
6. **Library of Congress, "The World of 1898" chronology
   (loc.gov/rr/hispanic/1898/chronology.html),** and period newspapers via
   Chronicling America. Good for sequence and for what was reported when.
7. **Secondary works,** for example Edmund Morris, *The Rise of Theodore
   Roosevelt* (1979), and Clay Risen, *The Crowded Hour* (2019). Use for
   leads and read the documents they cite.

## Known traps

- The fighting at the heights is written as "San Juan Hill." In prose use
  "Kettle Hill" or "San Juan Heights," and check which height a given unit took in
  the reports of operations. [house-rule]
- Unit names are miswritten (the regiment's official name, "Cavalry" as
  "Calvary," squadron names swapped for one another). Use the name in
  the official record. [method]
- Casualty figures for one day, for the whole campaign, and for killed versus
  killed and wounded are swapped. Say which list and which dates you mean.
  [method]
- Durations and intervals (the length of the war, days from landing to first
  fight, his time at the Navy Department) are estimated. Compute from the
  official start and end dates. [ref: F-20]
- Who ordered what is compressed: a famous cable and the later order that
  actually sent the fleet into action are merged, or a subordinate's orders
  are credited to T.R. Check the chain of command in the document record.
  [method]
- Battle narratives give T.R. tactical decisions his commanding officer made.
  Read the after-action reports and his own account side by side. [method]
- Fleet facts are mixed up: when the decision was made versus when the fleet
  sailed, the route around South America, and how the cruise was funded.
  Use the Navy's itinerary and T.R.'s own account. [ref: G-11]
- Ranks and offices are given for the wrong date (a colonel called that
  before promotion; an acting secretary called the secretary). Date the rank.
  [method]
- The date of a Medal of Honor award is misplaced, or a family member's
  medal is credited to him. Check the official recipient record and the date
  of award. [method]
- Cabled orders quoted with words added or "corrected." The printed cable is
  the text. [method]

## Procedure

1. Pin the claim to a date, a unit or ship, and a place.
2. Find the official record first: muster roll, casualty list, report of
   operations, Navy list or ship history, or the Medal of Honor record.
3. Add T.R.'s own account and one independent secondary or newspaper
   source. Note where his numbers differ from the official ones.
4. Compute every duration, count and age, and show the arithmetic.
5. Check the exact unit name, rank and title for that date.
6. Hand off family service, sequences, diplomacy and quotes as listed above.
7. Write the narrowest accurate correction, using "Kettle Hill" or "San Juan
   Heights" in prose.
