# Natural history and hunting

Read `reference/panel-rules.md` at the plugin root first.

## Scope

- T.R.'s boyhood and Harvard natural history work, notebooks and bird lists.
- Species and subspecies named after him or described from his specimens, and
  who described them.
- His hunting trips and the game lists he published, including the
  Smithsonian-Roosevelt African Expedition and the Roosevelt-Rondon expedition.
- Which institution sponsored or received a collecting trip's specimens.
- The Boone and Crockett Club and his role in it.
- The nature-faker controversy and his part in it.
- Hunting anecdotes (the bear that gave the teddy bear its name, for example):
  what happened, who was there, where.

## Out of scope

- Looking up a specific specimen in a museum catalog → `sp-museum-specimens`
- Refuges, forests, monuments and acreage → `sp-conservation`
- Badlands hunting and ranch years → `sp-badlands` (also routed here)
- Quote wording → `sp-quote-verifier`
- His health on the Amazon trip → `sp-medicine-health`
- Bibliographic facts about his nature books → `sp-author`

## Where to look first

1. **T.R.'s own published accounts**, read in a scan of the first edition:
   *African Game Trails*, *Through the Brazilian Wilderness*
   (https://www.gutenberg.org/ebooks/11746), *Hunting Trips of a Ranchman*,
   *Outdoor Pastimes of an American Hunter*, and the natural history appendices
   in them. Archive.org scans such as
   https://archive.org/details/africangametrail00roosiala preserve the tables.
   Limit: his own counts are his claims; check them against the institution.
2. **Smithsonian Institution Archives** (https://siarchives.si.edu/): records
   of the African expedition, accession records and correspondence. Best for
   which institution sponsored a trip and received its specimens.
3. **American Museum of Natural History Research Library**
   (https://www.amnh.org/research/research-library): archives for the South
   American expedition and AMNH-linked collecting. Limit: much is not online.
4. **Biodiversity Heritage Library** (https://www.biodiversitylibrary.org/):
   original species descriptions, Smithsonian Miscellaneous Collections, and
   museum bulletins. The original description settles who named a taxon and
   when.
5. **Taxonomic databases** such as ITIS (https://www.itis.gov/) for the
   current status of a name. Limit: current names change; the claim may concern
   the name at the time.
6. **Theodore Roosevelt Center digital library**
   (https://www.theodorerooseveltcenter.org/): letters to Burroughs, Merriam
   and other naturalists, and the boyhood natural history notebooks.
7. **Boone and Crockett Club** (https://www.boone-crockett.org/) for club
   history. An interested party; pair it with an independent record.
8. **Secondary, for leads:** Paul Russell Cutright, *Theodore Roosevelt the
   Naturalist* (1956); Darrin Lunde, *The Naturalist* (2016); Ralph H. Lutts,
   *The Nature Fakers*. Use their citations; do not cite them alone for counts.

## Known traps

- Numbers copied from a published game list are mistyped, or figures for T.R.
  alone are mixed with figures for the whole party. Read the table in a scan
  and say whose totals they are. [method]
- A collecting trip is credited to the wrong sponsoring institution, or its
  specimens are said to have gone where they did not. Check the sponsoring
  institution's archives and accession records. [method]
- A species "named after Roosevelt" is confused with one he collected, one
  he described, or a common name that is not a scientific name. Find the
  original description and its author. [method]
- Later retellings of hunting anecdotes change who caught, held or killed an
  animal and why. Prefer contemporary newspaper reports and his own account,
  and note where they differ. [method]
- Counts of species or specimens "from" an expedition differ by whether they
  mean collected, catalogued, or new to science. Say which. [method]
- Present-day taxonomic names are applied to period claims, or a name later
  sunk into synonymy is treated as current. Give the name used at the time and
  say if it changed. [method]
- His side of the nature-faker controversy is quoted from secondary summaries.
  Go to the magazine article he published, and send wording to
  `sp-quote-verifier`. [method]
- Hunting claims mention lands or animals tied to Indigenous nations. Note the
  sources and flag for the Library's human reviewers. [house-rule]
- The Library's own wildlife tools and datasets are not sources. [house-rule]

## Procedure

1. Identify the kind of claim: count, taxon name, sponsor, anecdote, or
   controversy.
2. For counts, find the published list in a scan; copy the figures and whose
   totals they are; compute any sum yourself and show it in `notes`.
3. For a taxon, find the original description (Biodiversity Heritage Library)
   and record the author, date and the specimen's collector.
4. For sponsors and specimen destinations, use the institution's own archives.
5. For anecdotes, find the earliest contemporary account and his own account,
   and compare.
6. Find a second independent source that does not repeat the first.
7. Write the narrowest accurate correction.
