# Legacy and commemoration

Read `reference/panel-rules.md` at the plugin root first.

## Scope

- Memorial organizations formed after his death: the Roosevelt Memorial
  Association, the Woman's Roosevelt Memorial Association, any successor
  bodies, and the Theodore Roosevelt Association.
- Memorials and monuments: statues, memorial halls, plaques, and their
  dedication, alteration, relocation or removal.
- Commemorative events: funerals and memorial services, centennials,
  sesquicentennials, anniversary programs.
- Federal and state commemorative acts, including national park and memorial
  designations and name changes.
- How anniversaries are dated and counted in Library copy.

## Out of scope

- Things named for him and honors awarded → `sp-honors-namesakes`
- The houses and cabins as historic sites → `sp-roosevelt-sites`
- How historians' views of T.R. changed → `sp-historiography`
- Myths and legends that grew after his death → `sp-myths`
- Debates over monuments as current events, and claims about living people →
  flag for the Library's human reviewers (panel rules section 10)

## Where to look first

1. **Theodore Roosevelt Association** (https://www.theodoreroosevelt.org/) and
   the TRA history documents in the **Theodore Roosevelt Center digital
   library** (https://www.theodorerooseveltcenter.org/): the association's own
   account of its predecessors. Institutional history; check dates against
   charters and newspapers.
2. **Theodore Roosevelt Collection, Harvard Library**
   (https://library.harvard.edu/collections/theodore-roosevelt-collection):
   its overview and finding aids document its own origins and memorial-era
   collecting.
3. **National Park Service administrative histories** (NPS History,
   https://npshistory.com/administrative_histories.htm) and park pages
   (for example https://www.nps.gov/thro/index.htm): designation and renaming
   history.
4. **Statutes and proclamations** (govinfo.gov, https://www.govinfo.gov/):
   exact wording and dates of commemorative and designation acts.
5. **Museum memorial histories**, such as the American Museum of Natural
   History's Theodore Roosevelt Memorial pages
   (https://www.amnh.org/exhibitions/permanent/theodore-roosevelt-memorial):
   construction, dedication and any later changes, from the holder.
6. **Period newspapers** read in the original (Chronicling America,
   https://www.loc.gov/collections/chronicling-america/about-this-collection/,
   for earlier decades;
   later papers through a library database): dedications and ceremonies.
7. **City, state and agency records** for monuments: commission minutes,
   permits, and removal or relocation decisions, read in the official record.

## Known traps

- Anniversaries are miscounted, or a "years since" figure is fixed to a draft
  date instead of the publication date. Compute from the event date to the date
  the copy will run, and show the arithmetic. [method]
- The dates of authorization, groundbreaking, completion and dedication of a
  memorial are confused. Name the milestone. [method]
- A removed or relocated monument is described as still standing where it was,
  or as destroyed when it was moved. Check the current official record. [method]
- Predecessor and successor organizations are treated as one body with one
  founding date. Give each organization's name for the period. [method]
- A park, memorial or site name in force today is used for a date before the
  name changed, or the reverse. Use the name in force at the time. [method]
- An organization's own history is the only source for its claims about
  itself. Find a charter, statute or newspaper report as the second source.
  [method]
- A centennial event is described from its program or announcement rather than
  a report that it happened as planned. [method]
- Library-produced anniversary lists, calendars and services are not sources.
  [house-rule]

## Procedure

1. Identify the memorial, organization, event or act and the date the claim
   gives.
2. Find the founding or authorizing document (charter, statute, proclamation,
   commission record).
3. Confirm what happened and when from a period report or official record.
4. For anniversaries, compute the count from the event date and the planned
   publication date; show it.
5. Check current status (standing, moved, removed, renamed) against the current
   holder or agency.
6. Write the narrowest accurate correction.
