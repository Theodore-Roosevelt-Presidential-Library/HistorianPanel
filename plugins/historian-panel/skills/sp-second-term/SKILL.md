# Second term, 1905-1909

Read `reference/panel-rules.md` at the plugin root first.

## Scope

- The 1905 inauguration and the second-term cabinet and appointments.
- Mediation of the Russo-Japanese War, the Portsmouth conference and treaty,
  and the Nobel Peace Prize: award, date, presentation and lecture.
- Domestic legislation: railroad rate regulation, the pure food and meat
  inspection laws, and related acts.
- The Brownsville affair: the incident, the investigation, the discharge order
  and later reviews.
- The Great White Fleet: the decision, departure, route, return and review.
- The Panic of 1907 and the administration's response.
- The 1908 succession: the choice of William Howard Taft, the convention, the
  election and the handover.

## Out of scope

- Diplomacy in depth (the treaty terms, Algeciras, the Gentlemen's Agreement,
  the Root-Takahira notes) → `sp-foreign-policy`
- Forests, monuments, refuges and acreage → `sp-conservation`
- Who held a cabinet or diplomatic post and for how long →
  `sp-officeholder-sequences`
- Firsts and "only" claims → `sp-superlatives`
- Quotations → `sp-quote-verifier`
- Photographs → `sp-photo-id`
- Race and the interpretation of the Brownsville affair → `sp-hard-chapters`
  (this specialist still checks its dates, numbers and orders)
- Anything after March 4, 1909 → `sp-africa-europe-bull-moose`

## Where to look first

1. **T.R.'s messages and papers:** annual and special messages through the
   American Presidency Project (https://www.presidency.ucsb.edu/), and
   *Presidential Addresses and State Papers* in Internet Archive
   (https://archive.org/) or HathiTrust (https://www.hathitrust.org/) scans.
2. **Library of Congress Theodore Roosevelt Papers**
   (https://www.loc.gov/collections/theodore-roosevelt-papers/) and the
   Theodore Roosevelt Center digital library
   (https://www.theodorerooseveltcenter.org/digital-library/) for dated
   letters.
3. **Nobel Prize records:** the official prize page
   (https://www.nobelprize.org/prizes/peace/1906/roosevelt/facts/) and the
   lecture page (https://www.nobelprize.org/prizes/peace/1906/roosevelt/lecture/),
   which separate the award year from the date and place of the lecture.
4. **Office of the Historian, U.S. Department of State:** the Portsmouth
   milestone (https://history.state.gov/milestones/1899-1913/portsmouth-treaty)
   and its Chiefs of Mission and people pages for diplomats.
5. **Naval History and Heritage Command, The Great White Fleet**
   (https://www.history.navy.mil/research/histories/ship-histories/the-great-white-fleet.html)
   and its linked itinerary pages for departure, ports and return.
6. **Statutes at Large** on govinfo
   (https://www.govinfo.gov/app/collection/statute) for each act's date of
   approval; the FDA History Office (https://www.fda.gov/about-fda/fda-history)
   for the food and drug law's background.
7. **Federal Reserve History, Panic of 1907**
   (https://www.federalreservehistory.org/essays/panic-of-1907) for the
   sequence of the panic; check its dates against period newspapers.
8. **Brownsville:** the War Department and Senate documents of 1906-1908 (in
   the Congressional Serial Set and National Archives records) and the 1972
   Army review; John D. Weaver, *The Brownsville Raid* (1970) as a secondary
   lead.

## Known traps

- The Nobel Prize, the Portsmouth treaty and the 1904 campaign are linked in
  the wrong order, or the prize is described as known or expected before it
  was awarded. Check the award year against the event being described.
  [ref: G-01]
- The year of the prize, the year of the lecture and the city of the lecture
  are merged. Use the official prize pages. [method]
- Diplomats and cabinet officers are given a post they held before or after
  the moment in question, or the wrong title for the post. Check the
  department's own list for each person. [ref: G-15, E-07]
- The Great White Fleet's route is described as passing through a canal that
  was not yet open, or its departure and return dates are swapped with the
  dates of its announcement. Use the NHHC itinerary. [ref: G-11]
- Brownsville numbers (soldiers discharged, companies, the kind of discharge)
  and dates (the incident, the order, its execution) are stated loosely.
  Check each against the order and the official documents. [method]
- The two 1906 food and meat laws are merged into one act or given each
  other's dates. Read the date of approval on each statute. [method]
- The Panic of 1907 is dated by its start, its worst days or its end without
  saying which. Name the milestone. [method]
- "Hand-picked Taft" is stated as a single decision on a single date. Look for
  the dated letters and announcements, and give the narrowest wording.
  [method]
- The Library's own timelines and datasets are not independent sources.
  [house-rule]

## Procedure

1. Pin the claim to a date or date range and to a milestone (announced,
   signed, departed, awarded, delivered).
2. Find the primary or official record for that milestone: statute, message,
   order, NHHC itinerary, Nobel record, FRUS or Senate document.
3. Check each person's office and title at that exact date.
4. Check that nothing in the claim depends on something that had not happened
   yet.
5. Hand diplomacy in depth, conservation, sequences, superlatives, quotes and
   images to their specialists; send interpretive Brownsville questions to
   `sp-hard-chapters`.
6. Compute any interval or count and show the arithmetic.
7. Write the narrowest accurate correction.
