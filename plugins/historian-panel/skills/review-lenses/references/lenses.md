# The lenses

Each lens is a set of questions. Ask all of them of the text; report only the
ones that turn up something.

## Narrative biographer

Cares whether the story is true as well as vivid.

- Is every scene (dialogue, weather, feelings, "he paused") traceable to a
  source, or is it embroidery?
- Are reported thoughts and emotions sourced to diaries or letters, or
  imagined?
- Does the arc (grief to rebirth, weakling to strongman) flatten a messier
  record?
- Are composite or legendary anecdotes presented as single events?

## Documentary editor

Cares about texts, dates and provenance.

- Is every quotation exact, with its source, date and edition?
- Is anything paraphrased inside quotation marks?
- Are letters and diary entries dated, and is the recipient named?
- Is a later recollection presented as a contemporary record?

## Political historian

Cares about context and stakes.

- Who were the other actors (Congress, party bosses, opponents), and are they
  missing?
- Is an outcome credited to T.R. alone that took votes, allies or compromise?
- Are motives asserted without evidence?
- Is a present-day political framing laid over the past?

## Critical historian

Cares about who is left out and what is softened.

- Whose perspective is absent (workers, Black Americans, Indigenous nations,
  immigrants, colonized peoples, women)?
- Is a hard chapter (Brownsville, the Philippines, views on race) skipped,
  softened, or sensationalized?
- Is period language quoted with enough context, and not repeated as the
  Library's own voice?
- Any claim touching Indigenous history goes to the Library's human
  reviewers.

## Regional historian

Cares about North Dakota and the Badlands as places with their own history.

- Is the landscape described accurately (buttes, not mesas; rivers, distances)?
- Are local people, towns and events named and placed correctly, rather than
  serving as backdrop for T.R.?
- Is Dakota Territory distinguished from the later state, and Montana Territory
  from Dakota?
- Are local sources (period newspapers, the State Historical Society) used
  where they exist?

## Military historian

Cares about units, ranks, chains of command and casualties.

- Are units, ranks and commanders right for the date?
- Is "he led" accurate, or did someone else command?
- Are casualty figures killed, wounded, or both?
- Are battles named and placed correctly (Kettle Hill, the San Juan Heights)?

## Naturalist

Cares about animals, specimens and science.

- Are species, numbers and places of collection right?
- Is hunting described accurately for its time, without modern spin either way?
- Are museum destinations of specimens right?
- Are scientific names and the naturalist's own records cited?

## Which lenses fit

| Subject | Suggested lenses |
|---------|------------------|
| Exhibit text, /tr hubs, press releases | all seven |
| Badlands, ranching | narrative, regional, documentary |
| Presidency, policy, elections | political, critical, documentary |
| War, Rough Riders, sons' service | military, narrative, critical |
| Conservation, hunting, safari, Amazon | naturalist, critical, regional |
| Family and personal life | narrative, documentary, critical |
| Quotes pages, social posts | documentary, narrative |
