# Medicine and health

Read `reference/panel-rules.md` at the plugin root first.

## Scope

- T.R.'s childhood asthma and other childhood illnesses, and what was done for
  them.
- His eyesight and the eye injury from boxing.
- Injuries and illnesses: accidents, the Amazon expedition, the bullet from
  the 1912 assassination attempt, and his last illnesses.
- Causes of death for T.R., Alice Hathaway Lee Roosevelt, Martha Bulloch
  Roosevelt, and other family members.
- Physicians named in the record and what they reported.

## Out of scope

- Wounds and medical discharges of his sons in war → `sp-family-offices`
- Who took part in the Amazon expedition and its route → `sp-last-years`
- Exercise, boxing and sport as activities → `sp-sports-strenuous-life`
- Quote wording → `sp-quote-verifier`
- Any present-day medical judgment about a living person → the Library's human
  reviewers

## Where to look first

1. **Contemporary family records**: T.R.'s diaries and letters and family
   letters (Library of Congress Theodore Roosevelt Papers,
   https://www.loc.gov/collections/theodore-roosevelt-papers/; Houghton Library
   Theodore Roosevelt Collection,
   https://library.harvard.edu/collections/theodore-roosevelt-collection;
   Theodore Roosevelt Center, https://www.theodorerooseveltcenter.org/).
2. **Death records and contemporary medical statements**: official death
   certificates and physicians' bulletins as printed in period newspapers
   (Chronicling America, https://www.loc.gov/collections/chronicling-america/).
   The period cause of death as recorded, in period terms.
3. **T.R.'s own accounts**: *An Autobiography*
   (https://www.gutenberg.org/ebooks/3335) for childhood health and the boxing
   injury; *Through the Brazilian Wilderness*
   (https://www.gutenberg.org/ebooks/11746) for the expedition. His accounts
   are his claims and can be modest or vague.
4. **National Park Service sites**: Theodore Roosevelt Birthplace
   (https://www.nps.gov/thrb/) and Sagamore Hill (https://www.nps.gov/sahi/)
   history pages, which cite the family record.
5. **History-of-medicine collections**: the National Library of Medicine
   (https://www.nlm.nih.gov/hmd/ and https://collections.nlm.nih.gov/) for what
   a period diagnosis or treatment meant at the time.
6. **Medical literature on T.R.** (PubMed, https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/):
   articles that review his history. These are retrospective interpretations;
   use them for leads and label them as interpretation.
7. **Secondary, for leads:** Candice Millard, *The River of Doubt* (2005) for
   the expedition; the standard biographies for childhood health. Follow their
   citations.

## Known traps

- A modern diagnosis is presented as what doctors said at the time. Give the
  period diagnosis in period terms, and label any modern reading as a
  retrospective interpretation, naming who proposed it. [method]
- A cause of death is stated more precisely than the record allows, or two
  different contemporary statements are blended. Quote what the record says
  and note where accounts differ. [method]
- Deaths and illnesses in the same household on the same day are compressed
  or rearranged (who died first, where, after how many days). Check each event
  separately against family letters, diaries and death records. [method]
- The timing of a diagnosis is moved: what was known before death is mixed
  with what was learned or named afterward. Say when each finding was made.
  [method]
- Later retellings change what he did after an injury (stopped an activity,
  carried on, switched to another). Read his own account and a contemporary
  record. [method]
- Details of the 1912 bullet (where it lodged, whether it was removed) are
  repeated from later popular accounts. Use the surgeons' contemporary
  statements as printed at the time. [method]
- Expedition illnesses are given as a single disease when the record shows
  several problems, or the other way round. List what the sources name.
  [method]
- Claims about inherited conditions or family medical patterns go beyond what
  any record shows. Return unverifiable rather than infer. [method]
- The Library's own family tree and timelines are not sources. [house-rule]

## Procedure

1. Identify the person, the condition or event, and the date.
2. Find the contemporary record first: diary, letter, physician's statement or
   death record, read in the original or a faithful scan.
3. Record the period term used. Do not translate it into a modern diagnosis in
   `corrected_text`.
4. Find a second independent contemporary or authoritative source.
5. If copy uses a modern diagnosis, check whether a named scholar or physician
   proposed it, and label it as retrospective.
6. Check the sequence and day of the week for every dated event.
7. Write the narrowest accurate correction.
