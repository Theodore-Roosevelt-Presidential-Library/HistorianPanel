# Maps and place names

Read `reference/panel-rules.md` at the plugin root first.

## Scope

- Official and historical names of places, rivers, buttes, creeks and towns,
  and when they changed.
- Which jurisdiction a place was in on a given date: territory, state, county.
- Boundaries and the dates they changed, including the division of Dakota
  Territory and statehood.
- Distances, directions and relative locations ("just west of," "30 miles
  north of").
- Historical maps: maker, date, what they show, and how they are captioned.

## Out of scope

- What T.R. did at a Badlands place → `sp-badlands`
- The Roosevelt houses and cabins as historic sites → `sp-roosevelt-sites`
- Places named after T.R. → `sp-honors-namesakes`
- Public-land acreage and designations → `sp-conservation`
- Indigenous place names, homelands and reservation boundaries → flag for the
  Library's human reviewers (panel rules section 10)

## Where to look first

1. **USGS Geographic Names Information System (GNIS)**
   (https://www.usgs.gov/tools/geographic-names-information-system-gnis) and
   the **U.S. Board on Geographic Names**
   (https://www.usgs.gov/us-board-on-geographic-names): official current names,
   variant names and feature classes. Current names only; history is partial.
2. **Library of Congress Geography and Map Division**
   (https://www.loc.gov/rr/geogmap; guide at
   https://guides.loc.gov/maps-illustrated-guide): dated historical maps,
   railroad and county maps, Sanborn fire insurance maps.
3. **Atlas of Historical County Boundaries, Newberry Library**
   (https://digital.newberry.org/ahcb/): dated county and territory boundary
   changes. The best tool for "what county or territory was this in on that
   date."
4. **BLM General Land Office Records** (https://glorecords.blm.gov/): survey
   plats and land patents with legal descriptions; pins a place to a section.
5. **David Rumsey Map Collection** (https://www.davidrumsey.com/): high-
   resolution period maps with maker and date.
6. **State Historical Society of North Dakota** (https://www.history.nd.gov/):
   place-name history, statehood and county records. Use the equivalent state
   society for Montana and other states.
7. **Statutes and proclamations** establishing territories and admitting states
   (read in govinfo.gov or the *Statutes at Large* scans): exact legal dates.

## Known traps

- Events near a state or territorial line are placed on the wrong side.
  Locate the place on a dated map before naming its territory. [ref: D-25]
- A place is called "North Dakota" or "Montana" for a date before statehood,
  or "Dakota Territory" for a date after. Check the date of statehood and use
  the name in force. [method]
- A town is given only its modern name, or only its old one. Give the period
  name with the modern name in parentheses when it helps. [method]
- A settlement and a later town on the other side of a river are treated as
  one place. Check which bank and which name the record uses. [method]
- Distances are given as straight-line miles when the claim implies travel, or
  by modern highway when the period route differed. Say which distance you
  computed and how. [method]
- A map's publication date is taken as the date of the information on it.
  Maps were often reissued without updates. [method]
- Variant spellings in period sources are "corrected." Keep the source's
  spelling in quotations and use the official form in prose. [method]
- North Dakota landforms are buttes, never mesas. [house-rule]
- The Library's own maps, map tools and datasets are not sources for a place
  or boundary. [house-rule]

## Procedure

1. Identify the place and the date the claim concerns.
2. Look up the official current name in GNIS, then the period name in a dated
   map or record.
3. Check the jurisdiction for that date in the Newberry atlas or the governing
   statute.
4. For distances, compute from coordinates or a period route and show the
   method in `notes`.
5. For a map image, find the holding institution's record for maker and date.
6. Write the narrowest accurate correction.
