# First term, 1901-1905

Read `reference/panel-rules.md` at the plugin root first.

## Scope

- The first months: the inherited McKinley cabinet, early messages to Congress.
- Antitrust: the Northern Securities suit, its filing, the lower-court ruling
  and the Supreme Court decision; other suits of the term.
- The anthracite coal strike: the White House conference, the commission, the
  settlement and the return to work.
- Panama as a first-term event: the Hay-Herrán and Hay-Bunau-Varilla treaties,
  the November 1903 revolution, recognition, the Canal Zone, the start of U.S.
  construction.
- New departments and bureaus created in the term.
- The 1904 Republican nomination, the campaign against Alton B. Parker, the
  results, and the election-night statement about a further term.

## Out of scope

- Diplomacy in depth (Venezuela, the Roosevelt Corollary, Alaska boundary,
  the canal negotiations' fine print) → `sp-foreign-policy`
- Forests, monuments, refuges, reclamation and acreage → `sp-conservation`
- Who held a cabinet post, in what order and for how long →
  `sp-officeholder-sequences`
- "First president to..." and "never before" → `sp-superlatives`
- Quotations → `sp-quote-verifier`
- Photographs and captions → `sp-photo-id`
- Anything after March 4, 1905 → `sp-second-term`

## Where to look first

1. **T.R.'s messages and papers:** annual messages and proclamations through the
   American Presidency Project (https://www.presidency.ucsb.edu/) and
   *Presidential Addresses and State Papers* in scans at the Internet Archive
   (https://archive.org/) or HathiTrust (https://www.hathitrust.org/). Cite the
   edition you read.
2. **Library of Congress Theodore Roosevelt Papers**
   (https://www.loc.gov/collections/theodore-roosevelt-papers/) and the
   Theodore Roosevelt Center digital library
   (https://www.theodorerooseveltcenter.org/digital-library/) for letters with
   exact dates.
3. **Court records** for antitrust: the Supreme Court opinion in *Northern
   Securities Co. v. United States*, 193 U.S. 197
   (https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/193/197/), which recites the
   procedural history. Lower-court filings and dates come from the record, not
   from summaries.
4. **Office of the Historian, U.S. Department of State:** the Panama Canal
   milestone (https://history.state.gov/milestones/1899-1913/panama-canal) and
   *Foreign Relations of the United States, 1903*
   (https://history.state.gov/historicaldocuments/frus1903) for the dated
   cables and treaty texts.
5. **Report of the Anthracite Coal Strike Commission** (1903), in library and
   Internet Archive scans, for the commission's membership, dates and award.
6. **Election returns:** National Archives Electoral College results
   (https://www.archives.gov/electoral-college/1904) and the American
   Presidency Project (https://www.presidency.ucsb.edu/statistics/elections/1904).
7. **Statutes:** the Statutes at Large on govinfo
   (https://www.govinfo.gov/app/collection/statute) for acts that created
   departments and bureaus; read the date of approval on the act.
8. **Secondary, for leads only:** Edmund Morris, *Theodore Rex* (2001); Lewis L.
   Gould, *The Presidency of Theodore Roosevelt* (1991; 2nd ed. 2011). Follow
   their notes.

## Known traps

- The date a suit was announced, the date it was filed and the date it was
  decided are blurred together. Name which milestone the copy means and find
  that one in the record. [ref: G-08]
- Electoral vote counts and "carried every state outside the South" phrasing
  are written from memory. Read the official tally and the state-by-state
  list before accepting any total or regional claim. [ref: G-02]
- Statements that no president before had done something (declined another
  term, used a power, took a trip) are made without checking earlier
  presidents. Route to `sp-superlatives` and look for counterexamples.
  [ref: G-06]
- Cabinet officers and colonial administrators are given the wrong dates of
  service or a title they held only before or after the moment in question.
  Check each office against the department's own list. [ref: G-18, G-19]
- A stock photograph is captioned as a different scene from the term (a cabinet
  meeting, a signing) than it shows. Match it to the holding institution's
  record. [ref: G-20]
- The coal strike's end is dated by different milestones (the commission's
  appointment, the union vote, the return to work, the award). Say which one
  you mean and cite it. [method]
- The Panama revolution, recognition and the treaty are compressed into one
  day or presented in the wrong order. Use the dated FRUS documents. [method]
- Later achievements (the finished canal, the Nobel Prize, later laws) are
  folded into first-term or 1904-campaign claims. Check that each item existed
  at the time the copy describes. [ref: G-01, G-11]
- The Library's own timelines and datasets are not independent sources.
  [house-rule]

## Procedure

1. Pin the claim to a date or date range and identify the milestone it
   describes (announced, filed, signed, decided, took effect).
2. Find the primary record for that milestone: message, court opinion, statute,
   FRUS document, commission report, official returns.
3. Check that every item in a list existed or had happened by the date the
   copy describes.
4. Hand diplomatic depth, conservation, officeholder sequences, superlatives,
   quotes and images to their specialists.
5. Compute any interval and show the arithmetic.
6. Write the narrowest accurate correction.
