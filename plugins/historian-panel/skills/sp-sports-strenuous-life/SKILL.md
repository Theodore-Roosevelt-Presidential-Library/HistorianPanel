# Sports and the strenuous life

Read `reference/panel-rules.md` at the plugin root first.

## Scope

- Sports T.R. practiced: boxing, wrestling, jiu-jitsu, singlestick, tennis,
  rowing, riding, polo, hiking, swimming, point-to-point walks, and who his
  partners and instructors were.
- The "Tennis Cabinet" and other circles built around exercise.
- Period phrases tied to his circle and habits, such as the Ananias Club:
  where and when the phrase appears in the record and who used it.
- The "Strenuous Life" speech: occasion, date, venue, and later book.
- Football: any White House meeting on the game, who attended, what
  followed, and any family connection to college football.
- Physical culture: his advocacy of exercise and outdoor life, and youth
  athletics.

## Out of scope

- Injuries from sport (the eye injury, accidents) and their medical effects →
  `sp-medicine-health`
- Exact wording of speeches and letters → `sp-quote-verifier`
- Book and edition facts for *The Strenuous Life* → `sp-author`
- Hunting → `sp-natural-history`
- Ranch work and riding in Dakota → `sp-badlands`
- "First president to ..." claims → `sp-superlatives` (also routed here)

## Where to look first

1. **T.R.'s own accounts**: *An Autobiography*
   (https://www.gutenberg.org/ebooks/3335), especially its chapters on boyhood
   and outdoor life; *The Strenuous Life* essays
   (https://www.gutenberg.org/ebooks/58821); and his letters. His account is
   his claim; check dates and names against a second record.
2. **Theodore Roosevelt Center** (https://www.theodorerooseveltcenter.org/):
   letters, scrapbooks and its encyclopedia entries on sport and football.
   Follow the entries back to the documents they cite.
3. **Period newspapers** read in the original (Chronicling America,
   https://www.loc.gov/collections/chronicling-america/) for the date of a
   meeting, a walk or a match, and who was present.
4. **College newspapers and archives**: *The Harvard Crimson* archive
   (https://www.thecrimson.com/) and the Yale and Princeton archives, for
   football meetings, rules changes and injuries in the season the claim concerns.
5. **LA84 Foundation Digital Library** (https://digital.la84.org/) for
   historical sporting periodicals and early athletic governance records.
6. **National Park Service sites**: Sagamore Hill (https://www.nps.gov/sahi/)
   and Theodore Roosevelt Birthplace (https://www.nps.gov/thrb/), which cite
   family records on his exercise regime.
7. **Secondary, for leads:** Ryan Swanson, *The Strenuous Life: Theodore
   Roosevelt and the Making of the American Athlete* (2019). Use its
   citations.

## Known traps

- The order of events around a football meeting is reversed, or a single
  injury is made its cause. Date the meeting and each injury separately from
  contemporary reports. [method]
- The list of who attended a meeting (schools, coaches, advisers, presidents
  of colleges) is guessed or inflated. Use a contemporary report that names
  them. [method]
- His role in founding a later athletic body is overstated. Separate what he
  urged from what others organized, with dates. [method]
- Later retellings change whether he stopped a sport after an injury or simply
  changed sports. Read his own account first. [method]
- The "Strenuous Life" speech and the later book collection of the same name
  are confused (date, venue, contents). Cite the speech occasion for the speech
  and the book edition for the book. [method]
- Walk and match anecdotes are told with invented companions, distances or
  places. Use a contemporary newspaper or a participant's dated letter, and
  label later memoirs as recalled. [method]
- Instructors and sparring partners are named from popular articles. Find
  them in his letters or a contemporary record. [method]
- "Teddy" appears in copy about his sporting persona. Use "T.R." [house-rule]
- The Library's own tools and the Story Guide are not sources. [house-rule]

## Procedure

1. Identify the sport or event, the date and the people named.
2. Find his own account and a contemporary record (newspaper, letter,
   college paper).
3. For meetings and matches, date them from the contemporary report and check
   the day of the week.
4. List the people present only as a source names them.
5. Separate his own actions from what others did afterward, with dates.
6. Confirm with a second independent source, and label memoir material as
   recalled.
7. Write the narrowest accurate correction.
