# Harvard and Alice Lee, 1876-1880

Read `reference/panel-rules.md` at the plugin root first.

## Scope

- T.R. at Harvard College: courses, grades, honors, clubs, publications,
  friends and teachers, and his graduation.
- The illness and death of his father, and its effect on T.R. as the sources
  record it.
- Meeting, courting, becoming engaged to and marrying Alice Hathaway Lee; the
  Lee and Saltonstall families as they bear on that story; the wedding and the
  couple's first years.
- Columbia Law School: enrollment, study and leaving.
- Early writing: *The Naval War of 1812* and its editions, and other early
  publications.

## Out of scope

- The February 1884 deaths and the State Assembly → `sp-albany-assembly`
- Offices and careers of T.R.'s father, siblings and in-laws → 
  `sp-family-offices`
- Lineage and cousin degrees → `sp-generations`
- Quotes, including diary and letter lines → `sp-quote-verifier` (also routed
  here)
- Photographs of T.R., Alice or the Lee family → `sp-photo-id`
- Naval history claims in the book that are not about the book itself → flag;
  no specialist yet
- "First" and "only" claims → `sp-superlatives`

## Where to look first

1. **T.R.'s diaries and letters for 1876-1880:** Library of Congress, Theodore
   Roosevelt Papers (https://www.loc.gov/collections/theodore-roosevelt-papers/)
   and Theodore Roosevelt Center scans
   (https://www.theodorerooseveltcenter.org/digital-library/). Dated entries
   settle when he met, courted and became engaged to Alice.
2. **Harvard University Archives**
   (https://library.harvard.edu/libraries/harvard-university-archives):
   catalogues, class lists, commencement programs and class reports. The
   official record for courses, honors and degrees. Search finding aids in
   HOLLIS for Archival Discovery (https://hollisarchives.lib.harvard.edu/).
3. **Houghton Library, Theodore Roosevelt Collection**
   (https://library.harvard.edu/collections/theodore-roosevelt-collection):
   family papers and photographs of the college years.
4. **Columbia University Archives**
   (https://library.columbia.edu/libraries/cuarchives.html): enrollment records
   for the law school years. Ask what exists before claiming a date of entry or
   departure.
5. **The Naval War of 1812, first edition,** read in a scan at the Internet
   Archive (https://archive.org/) or HathiTrust (https://www.hathitrust.org/);
   Project Gutenberg #9104 (https://www.gutenberg.org/ebooks/9104) is a
   convenient text but check which edition it reproduces.
6. **Massachusetts Historical Society** (https://www.masshist.org/): papers of
   T.R.'s Boston circle, including Henry Cabot Lodge. Good for when and how
   friendships began.
7. **Secondary, for leads only:** David McCullough, *Mornings on Horseback*
   (1981); Edmund Morris, *The Rise of Theodore Roosevelt* (1979). Trace each
   fact to the document they cite.

## Known traps

- Where and when he met Alice is folded into a later chapter of his life
  (politics, New York). Pin the meeting to the diary and keep it separate from
  what came after the marriage. [ref: A-34]
- A friend's or teacher's position is given as it was later, not as it was
  when T.R. knew him. Check the person's title for the exact year in an
  official catalogue or the person's own papers. [ref: D-27]
- His father's influence is described with "always" and "every time he was
  asked," and the years of later public life it touched are miscounted. Keep
  to what T.R. wrote, and compute spans from dates. [ref: B-11]
- The book's first edition, later revised editions and its later official use
  are blended into one claim. Name the edition and year you are citing.
  [method]
- Courtship milestones (first meeting, proposal, acceptance, public
  announcement, wedding) are merged or reordered. Give each its own dated
  source. [method]
- Honors and club memberships are stated from memory or biography. Confirm
  against Harvard's own published records or the club's records. [method]
- Ages and day-of-week claims around the wedding and his father's death are
  estimated. Compute them and show the arithmetic. [method]
- The Library's own timelines and family trees are not independent sources.
  [house-rule]

## Procedure

1. Pin the claim to a date, a place and the people named.
2. Check T.R.'s own dated diary entry or letter first.
3. For academic claims, confirm in Harvard's or Columbia's institutional
   record; for the book, in the first edition itself.
4. For people around him, check their own papers or an official list for the
   exact year.
5. Compute ages, spans and days of the week from the sources.
6. When sources disagree, give the narrowest wording all good sources support
   and say so in `notes`.
7. Write the narrowest accurate correction.
