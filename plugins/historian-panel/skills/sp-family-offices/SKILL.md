# Family offices and careers

Read `reference/panel-rules.md` at the plugin root first.

## Scope

Claims about what members of T.R.'s family did: offices, appointments, military
service and rank, decorations, schooling, careers, marriages, births and deaths,
and where they lived. Also which relative is meant when names repeat across
generations.

## Out of scope

- Generation counts and cousin degrees → `sp-generations`
- Who is in a photograph → `sp-photo-id`
- Exact wording of anything a relative said or wrote → `sp-quote-verifier`
- "First" or "only" claims about a relative → `sp-superlatives` (also routed here)

## Where to look first

1. **Biographical Directory of the U.S. Congress** (bioguide.congress.gov). The
   check for any claim that someone served in the House or Senate. No entry is
   strong evidence they did not; record the search.
2. **U.S. Department of State, Office of the Historian** (history.state.gov).
   Diplomats and the exact chief-of-mission title on a given date.
3. **Military records:** National Museum of the U.S. Army biographies; Medal of
   Honor citations (U.S. Army Center of Military History); American Battle
   Monuments Commission and Arlington burial records, which state rank at death;
   National Archives service records.
4. **National Park Service "people" pages** (nps.gov/people) and park history
   pages for Sagamore Hill, the Birthplace, and Theodore Roosevelt National Park.
5. **Theodore Roosevelt Center encyclopedia** (theodorerooseveltcenter.org,
   Dickinson State University), especially "Family and Friends."
6. **Official lists of territorial and colonial officials** for titles such as
   governor and governor-general.
7. **Period newspapers** (Chronicling America, plus licensed archives if you
   have them) for appointment, wedding and death dates. Use the article's text,
   not its issue date, for the event date.
8. **Standard biographies** for corroboration. Cite edition and page.

## Known traps

- Offices are inflated or invented for T.R.'s sons and relatives, including
  service in Congress. Confirm every office against an official list. [ref: C-0001]
- Titles that changed by date or place get swapped: governor vs.
  governor-general, civil governor before the later title existed, minister vs.
  ambassador. Match the title to the date. [ref: A-01, G-15, G-19, E-07]
- Promotions are claimed that never took effect, including "posthumous"
  promotions. Rank at death comes from the burial record and citation.
  [ref: A-02, C-16]
- Military service gets compressed: "commissioned as" a rank first reached by
  promotion, or a unit assigned late in a war placed at its start.
  [ref: A-04, C-19, A-18]
- Wounds and battles get reassigned between wars or engagements. Tie each to a
  dated record. [ref: C-17, A-03, A-13]
- "Jr." is ambiguous. T.R. used it until his father died; elsewhere it usually
  means his son. Name the person with birth year in your notes. [ref: A-33]
- Family relationships are mislabeled: half-sister vs. stepsister, "elder
  daughter" when there was one daughter by that marriage. [ref: A-26, A-27]
- Ages and spans are miscounted. Compute from birth and event dates and show
  the arithmetic. [ref: A-13, A-14, C-03, C-22]
- Business milestones are placed in the wrong decade. Date firm foundings from
  the firm's own history or a trade publication. [ref: A-15]
- Names repeat across generations (Theodore, Kermit, Archibald, Alice). Pin
  every claim to one person before searching. [method]

## Procedure

1. Identify the person (full name and birth year) and restate the claim: the
   office or event, the title, and the date or date range.
2. Search the sources above in order until you have two independent ones, at
   least one official or primary.
3. For any title, confirm the title as it was on that date.
4. For any age or span, compute it and show the computation.
5. For negative claims, record where you looked.
6. Write the narrowest accurate correction if the claim is wrong.
