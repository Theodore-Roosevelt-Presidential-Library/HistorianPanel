# Progressive Era party politics

Read `reference/panel-rules.md` at the plugin root first.

## Scope

- Republican Party factions and leaders, 1900-1916, and where T.R. stood among
  them.
- Nominations and conventions: 1904, 1908, 1912 (the Republican convention,
  credentials fights, the Progressive convention) and 1916.
- Presidential preference primaries in 1912: which states held them, who won
  them, and what they did and did not decide.
- The Progressive (Bull Moose) Party: founding, platform planks, candidates,
  later fortunes.
- Election results: electoral votes, popular votes and percentages, states
  carried, third-party and Socialist totals.
- T.R.'s relationships with Taft and Wilson as political events (breaks,
  reconciliations, campaign attacks).
- Third-term questions, including his 1904 election-night statement and how it
  was later read.

## Out of scope

- Who held a cabinet or party office and in what order → `sp-officeholder-sequences`
- "First," "only," "never before" claims about elections → `sp-superlatives`
  (also routed here for the political record)
- The 1912 assassination attempt in Milwaukee and the campaign trail as
  biography → `sp-africa-europe-bull-moose`
- Quotes from speeches, including the Progressive convention address →
  `sp-quote-verifier`
- Policy content of the platform as later law → `sp-domestic-policy`
- Campaign cartoons and newsreels → `sp-film-sound-cartoons`

## Where to look first

1. **National Archives, Electoral College results by year**
   (https://www.archives.gov/electoral-college/1912, and the same pattern for
   1904, 1908, 1916): official electoral votes by state. Not popular votes.
2. **The American Presidency Project, election statistics**
   (https://www.presidency.ucsb.edu/statistics/elections/1912) and **party
   platforms** (https://www.presidency.ucsb.edu/documents/app-categories/elections-and-transitions/party-platforms):
   popular and electoral tables and platform texts. Popular totals are compiled
   and can differ slightly from other compilations; say which you used.
3. **Office of the Clerk, U.S. House, Election Statistics**
   (https://history.house.gov/Institution/Election-Statistics/Election-Statistics/):
   the Clerk's official statistics, issued per election. Good tie-breaker for
   popular votes.
4. **Official proceedings of the conventions** (Republican and Progressive
   national conventions, published after each) read in scans on HathiTrust or the
   Internet Archive: delegate counts, roll calls, credentials decisions.
5. **Period newspapers** in original pages through Chronicling America
   (https://www.loc.gov/collections/chronicling-america/about-this-collection/): day-by-day convention
   and primary reporting. Partisan papers slant counts; compare two.
6. **T.R.'s letters** (Library of Congress Theodore Roosevelt Papers,
   https://www.loc.gov/collections/theodore-roosevelt-papers/; Theodore
   Roosevelt Center, https://www.theodorerooseveltcenter.org/) for what he
   intended and when.
7. **Miller Center presidential essays** (millercenter.org): reliable overviews
   of each campaign. Secondary; use for orientation, not figures.
8. **Standard secondary works** (secondary): Lewis L. Gould, *Four Hats in the
   Ring* (2008), on 1912; John Milton Cooper Jr., *The Warrior and the Priest*
   (1983), on T.R. and Wilson.

## Known traps

- Sweeping summaries of a map ("every state outside the South," "every Northern
  state") skip the exceptions. Check every state, including split electoral
  votes within a state. [ref: G-02]
- An achievement or honor is listed as a campaign talking point before it
  happened. Check that each item predates the campaign it is attached to.
  [ref: G-01]
- "No president had ever" claims about declining or renouncing another term
  ignore earlier presidents who did so. Search the earlier record before
  accepting. [ref: G-06]
- Popular-vote figures vary between compilations, and percentages are computed
  on different bases (with or without minor parties). Give the source and
  compute the percentage yourself. [method]
- Primaries in 1912 are described as deciding the nomination without checking
  how many delegates were chosen by primary and how many by conventions and
  caucuses. Count both from the record. [method]
- Delegate totals at the 1912 Republican convention are quoted from one
  faction's claims. Use the official proceedings and say whose count it is.
  [method]
- The Progressive Party's later history is collapsed into its founding year.
  Date each event separately from the record. [method]
- "Bull Moose" is used as if it were the official name of the party. Use the
  official name for formal claims and note the nickname. [method]
- Election Day, the electoral vote count in Congress and inauguration are
  different dates. Say which one the claim means. [method]

## Procedure

1. Identify the election year and the exact quantity or event (electoral vote,
   popular vote, delegates, primary result, convention vote).
2. For figures, read the official table (National Archives or the House Clerk)
   and one independent compilation. Show any arithmetic.
3. For map summaries, list every state's result before accepting a sweeping
   statement.
4. For convention events, use the published proceedings and a period newspaper
   read in the original.
5. For intent or motive, find T.R.'s own letter or statement of the time and
   label later recollections as recalled.
6. Check the order of events so nothing is credited before it happened.
7. Write the narrowest accurate correction.
