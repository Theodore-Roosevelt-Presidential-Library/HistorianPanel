# Return east, 1886-1889

Read `reference/panel-rules.md` at the plugin root first.

## Scope

- The 1886 New York City mayoral campaign: his nomination, the other
  candidates, the result and the official returns.
- His engagement and marriage to Edith Kermit Carow, the wedding
  party and the trip that followed.
- The Sagamore Hill household: moving in, daughter Alice's place in it, and
  children born in these years.
- Books and articles he wrote or published, 1886-1889, and their editions.
- His public and political activity before his appointment to the Civil
  Service Commission.

## Out of scope

- Anything in Dakota or Montana, including ranch business and the winter of
  1886-87 → `sp-badlands`
- The Civil Service Commission and after → `sp-civil-service-police`
- Edith's own life beyond this marriage and household → `sp-family-offices`;
  her own figure, once added → `sp-edith`
- Children's later careers and life events → `sp-family-offices`
- Who ran for or held an office, in order → `sp-officeholder-sequences`
- Quotes → `sp-quote-verifier` (also routed here)
- Photographs → `sp-photo-id`

## Where to look first

1. **T.R.'s letters and diary for 1886-1889:** Library of Congress, Theodore
   Roosevelt Papers (https://www.loc.gov/collections/theodore-roosevelt-papers/)
   and Theodore Roosevelt Center scans
   (https://www.theodorerooseveltcenter.org/digital-library/). The edited
   *Letters of Theodore Roosevelt*, ed. Elting E. Morison (1951-54), for
   transcriptions; read the scan when wording matters.
2. **Period New York newspapers, read on the page:** Chronicling America
   (https://www.loc.gov/collections/chronicling-america/) and NYS Historic
   Newspapers (https://nyshistoricnewspapers.org/). Partisan papers framed the
   1886 race very differently; note which paper you used.
3. **NYC Municipal Archives** (https://www.archives.nyc/): city records,
   including the official canvass of votes. Newspaper election-night figures
   are not the official count.
4. **The parish marriage register** for the church where they married (find
   which archive holds it), and the civil registration
   record. Use these for date, church and witnesses rather than a biography.
5. **National Park Service, Sagamore Hill National Historic Site**
   (https://www.nps.gov/sahi/): history pages on the house, the family and the
   estate.
6. **First editions of his books of this period,** read in scans at HathiTrust
   (https://www.hathitrust.org/) or the Internet Archive (https://archive.org/),
   for title, publisher, year and series. Project Gutenberg
   (https://www.gutenberg.org/ebooks/author/2074) is a text, not a
   bibliographic record.
7. **Secondary, for leads only:** Sylvia Jukes Morris, *Edith Kermit
   Roosevelt: Portrait of a First Lady* (1980); Edmund Morris, *The Rise of
   Theodore Roosevelt* (1979).

## Known traps

- The wedding is given in vague, romantic terms or with the wrong church.
  Name the church and the date from the register or an authoritative entry.
  [ref: B-05]
- Spans between the February 1884 deaths and the second marriage are rounded
  down or up ("barely two years," "three years"). Compute from both dates.
  [ref: D-20, B-17]
- The career sequence from ranch life to Washington is compressed or
  reordered, skipping the mayoral race or the Commission years. Lay out each
  step with its own date. [ref: D-19]
- Birth order is counted without his first child, or birthplaces are assumed.
  Say whether a count includes daughter Alice, and confirm each birthplace.
  [ref: A-09]
- His finish in the mayoral race is stated from memory, or newspaper counts
  are used as the official result. Cite the canvass. [method]
- Book dates are taken from a later reprint or copyright page. Use the first
  edition's title page and a catalog record. [method]
- Claims that mix Badlands and Oyster Bay in one sentence need both
  specialists. Split the claim. [method]
- The Library's own timelines and family trees are not independent sources.
  [house-rule]

## Procedure

1. Pin the claim to a date, a place and the people involved.
2. Look first for T.R.'s own dated letter or diary entry.
3. For the election, use the official canvass and then newspapers read on the
   page; for the wedding, the register; for books, the first edition.
4. Compute ages, spans and days of the week from the sources.
5. Send any Dakota detail to `sp-badlands` and any later-career detail to
   `sp-civil-service-police`.
6. When sources disagree, give the narrowest wording all good sources support
   and say so in `notes`.
7. Write the narrowest accurate correction.
