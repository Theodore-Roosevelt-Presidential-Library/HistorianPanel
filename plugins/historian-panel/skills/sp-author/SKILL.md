# T.R. as author

Read `reference/panel-rules.md` at the plugin root first.

## Scope

- His books: title and subtitle as printed, publisher, place, year, edition,
  co-author or collaborator.
- Essays, magazine articles, reviews and editorials: venue, issue date,
  signed or unsigned.
- Serializations: which magazine ran a book first, and when.
- Collections of speeches and essays assembled in his lifetime and after.
- The collected Works editions and what each contains.
- Counts of his books and articles, and what the count includes.
- Pen names and unsigned pieces.

## Out of scope

- Wording of a passage → `sp-quote-verifier`
- Books written about him (modern biographies, recommended reading) →
  `sp-book-citations`
- Whether the facts in his books are true → the subject specialist
  (`sp-natural-history`, `sp-badlands`, `sp-conservation` and others)
- "First," "only," "most prolific" claims → `sp-superlatives` (also routed
  here)

## Where to look first

1. **The title page and copyright page** of the first edition, in a scan at
   the Internet Archive (https://archive.org/) or HathiTrust
   (https://catalog.hathitrust.org/). Settles title, publisher, place and year.
   Limit: a scanned later printing can carry the first edition's date.
2. **Library catalogs of record**: the Library of Congress catalog
   (https://catalog.loc.gov/) and WorldCat (https://search.worldcat.org/) for
   editions, printings and uniform titles. Catalog dates can be inferred; read
   the bracketed notes.
3. **John Hall Wheelock, *A Bibliography of Theodore Roosevelt* (Scribner,
   1920)**: the standard early bibliography of his books and articles. Limit:
   it predates later discoveries; check against catalogs.
4. **Theodore Roosevelt Center** (https://www.theodorerooseveltcenter.org/),
   including its bibliography page and digital library scans of articles and
   manuscripts.
5. **Houghton Library, Theodore Roosevelt Collection**
   (https://library.harvard.edu/collections/theodore-roosevelt-collection):
   manuscripts, proofs and the collection's printed bibliographic aids.
6. **Full-text editions** at Project Gutenberg (for example
   https://www.gutenberg.org/ebooks/3335) and The Online Books Page
   (https://onlinebooks.library.upenn.edu/). Good for finding a chapter; cite
   the printed edition the e-text reproduces, not the e-text date.
7. **The magazines themselves** (*Scribner's*, *The Century*, *The Outlook*,
   *Metropolitan* and others) in scanned volumes, for serialization dates and
   signed versus unsigned pieces.
8. **The collected Works editions** in scans, for what was collected and how
   it was retitled. Treat their texts as later printings.

## Known traps

- A passage is dated and titled by a later pamphlet or compilation that
  reprinted it, not by the speech or article where it first appeared. Find
  the earliest printing and date it from that. [ref: H-06, H-07]
- The copyright year, the printing year in a scan, and the first publication
  year are treated as the same. Read the title page and copyright page of a
  first edition. [method]
- Magazine serialization and book publication are merged, so a book is dated
  by its serial run or the serial is dated by the book. Give both, labeled.
  [method]
- Counts of "books he wrote" differ by whether they include speech
  collections, co-authored works, posthumous compilations and multi-volume
  works counted once or per volume. Say what the count includes. [method]
- A title is modernized, shortened or merged with its subtitle, or a
  collected-Works volume title is given as the original book title. Copy the
  title as printed. [method]
- A co-authored or collaborative book is credited to T.R. alone, or his
  contribution to someone else's book is listed as his book. Check the title
  page for every name. [method]
- Unsigned editorials or pieces signed with a pseudonym are attributed to him
  without evidence. Require a bibliography or a letter that identifies the
  piece as his. [method]
- An e-text's release date or a reprint publisher is cited as the publication
  date. [method]
- The Library's own reading lists and book datasets are not sources.
  [house-rule]

## Procedure

1. Identify the work and what the claim asserts (title, year, publisher,
   edition, venue, count).
2. Find a scan of the first edition or first printing and read the title and
   copyright pages.
3. Check the Library of Congress catalog or WorldCat for the edition record.
4. For articles, find the magazine issue itself and note the date, volume and
   pages.
5. For counts, state the rule for what counts, build the list from Wheelock
   and the catalogs, count it yourself, and show it in `notes`.
6. Confirm with a second independent source.
7. Write the narrowest accurate correction, with title as printed, publisher
   and year.
