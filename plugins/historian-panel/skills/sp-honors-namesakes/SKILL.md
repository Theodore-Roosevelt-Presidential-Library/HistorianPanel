# Honors and namesakes

Read `reference/panel-rules.md` at the plugin root first.

## Scope

- Honors T.R. received in life or after death: the Nobel Peace Prize, the
  Medal of Honor, honorary degrees, foreign decorations, society medals.
- Things named for him: rivers and other features, ships, schools, towns,
  counties, parks and preserves, buildings, awards, species.
- The teddy bear: the story behind the name and the first toys.
- Stamps, coins and commemorative medals bearing his image.
- His likeness on Mount Rushmore as an honor (selection, carving dates).
- Who did the naming or awarding, and on what date.

## Out of scope

- The Roosevelt houses and cabins as sites → `sp-roosevelt-sites`
- Memorials, monuments and anniversary events after his death →
  `sp-legacy-commemoration`
- The battle actions behind the Medal of Honor → `sp-navy-rough-riders`
- The physical medals and where they are now → `sp-objects`
- Public lands he designated (not named for him) → `sp-conservation`
- "First" or "only" recipient claims → `sp-superlatives`
- The cartoon behind the teddy bear → `sp-film-sound-cartoons`

## Where to look first

1. **The naming or awarding instrument:** the act of Congress, proclamation,
   decree, board decision, citation or award announcement, read in the
   original (govinfo.gov for federal law; the issuing body's records otherwise).
2. **Nobel Prize official site**
   (https://www.nobelprize.org/prizes/peace/1906/roosevelt/facts/): award year,
   motivation and presentation; the prize year and ceremony date can differ.
3. **U.S. Board on Geographic Names and GNIS**
   (https://www.usgs.gov/us-board-on-geographic-names): official names of
   features in the United States and their decision history.
4. **Naval History and Heritage Command, Dictionary of American Naval Fighting
   Ships** (history.navy.mil): ships named for T.R. or his family, with
   commissioning dates.
5. **Theodore Roosevelt Center** (https://www.theodorerooseveltcenter.org/):
   subject pages such as the Medal of Honor page gather the documents.
6. **Postal and coin records:** USPS postal history
   (https://about.usps.com/who/profile/history/), the Smithsonian National
   Postal Museum collections, and the U.S. Mint program pages (for example
   https://www.usmint.gov/coins/coin-medal-programs/presidential-dollar-coin/theodore-roosevelt).
7. **NCAA Theodore Roosevelt Award page**
   (https://www.ncaa.org/sports/2016/7/14/theodore-roosevelt-award.aspx): award
   purpose and recipients.
8. **National Park Service, Mount Rushmore** (https://www.nps.gov/moru/index.htm)
   and park history pages: carving chronology. For Brazilian names, T.R.'s
   *Through the Brazilian Wilderness* and Brazilian government records.

## Known traps

- An honor is mentioned as already held at a date before it was awarded.
  Check the award date against the date of the sentence it appears in.
  [ref: G-01]
- The date a thing was named is confused with the date the thing began (a ship
  launched versus commissioned, a school founded versus renamed, a river
  explored versus officially renamed). Give the date of naming. [method]
- A river or feature is said to have been renamed by a body that did not do
  the renaming, or the official name is confused with a local or informal one.
  Find the naming authority's own record. [method]
- Posthumous honors are written as if received in life, or the award date is
  confused with the date of the deed. Give both. [method]
- "Named for" is assumed from a name alone. A place named Roosevelt may honor
  someone else or the family generally; find the naming record. [method]
- The teddy bear story is told with details that grew in retelling. Trace each
  detail to a period account and say which are later. [method]
- Mount Rushmore dates (dedication of the memorial, start of carving, each
  face's dedication, end of work) are mixed. Name the milestone. [method]
- Write T.R., never "Teddy," except in the name of the teddy bear itself or a
  quoted title. [house-rule]

## Procedure

1. Identify the honor or namesake and the date or authority the claim gives.
2. Find the naming or awarding instrument and read it.
3. Separate the dates: deed, decision, announcement, ceremony, physical object.
4. Confirm the honoree is T.R. himself and not a relative or namesake.
5. Hand firsts and onlys to `sp-superlatives`.
6. Write the narrowest accurate correction.
