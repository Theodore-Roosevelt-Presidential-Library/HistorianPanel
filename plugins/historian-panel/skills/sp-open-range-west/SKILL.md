# The open-range cattle West

Read `reference/panel-rules.md` at the plugin root first.

## Scope

- The cattle business on the northern plains in the 1880s: investment,
  prices, herd sizes, foreign capital, beef bonanza promotion.
- Weather and range disasters, especially the winter of 1886-87, and claims
  about losses.
- Stock growers' associations (Little Missouri, Montana, Wyoming), brands,
  roundups and range law.
- The Northern Pacific Railroad: route, towns, land grants, shipping cattle.
- Vigilance committees and frontier justice.
- Frontier towns, and territorial status, boundaries and statehood dates for
  Dakota and Montana.

## Out of scope

- T.R.'s own ranches, partners and actions → `sp-badlands`
- His neighbors, foremen and partners as people → `sp-badlands-circle`
- Reliability of Badlands books and reminiscences →
  `sp-badlands-scholarship`
- Indigenous history, reservations, treaties and land cessions → flag for the
  Library's human reviewers via `sp-northern-plains-indigenous` (panel rules
  section 10)
- Wildlife and hunting → `sp-natural-history`

## Where to look first

1. **Association and company records:** Wyoming Stock Growers Association
   records at the American Heritage Center, University of Wyoming (finding aid
   on Archives West, archiveswest.orbiscascade.org); Northern Pacific Railway
   Company records at the Minnesota Historical Society (mnhs.org/library;
   railroad research guide at libguides.mnhs.org/railroad/trm). Best for
   dates, rules and shipping; large and only partly digitized.
2. **State Historical Society of North Dakota** (history.nd.gov/archives) and
   **Montana Historical Society** research center (mhs.mt.gov/research) for
   territorial, county and brand records.
3. **Federal land and census records:** BLM General Land Office records
   (glorecords.blm.gov) for patents and surveys; National Archives land and
   census guides (archives.gov/research/land, archives.gov/research/census).
4. **Period newspapers in the original pages:** Chronicling America
   (chroniclingamerica.loc.gov), Digital Horizons for North Dakota
   (digitalhorizonsonline.org), and Montana newspapers. Stock reports, weather
   and loss estimates as printed at the time.
5. **NPS studies** of Theodore Roosevelt National Park, especially the
   historic resource study and Mattison's articles on the stockmen's
   association (npshistory.com/publications/thro/index.htm). Secondary with
   notes.
6. **Standard secondary works:** Ernest Staples Osgood, *The Day of the
   Cattleman* (1929); Lewis Atherton, *The Cattle Kings* (1961); Granville
   Stuart, *Forty Years on the Frontier* (1925, a memoir). Secondary or
   reminiscence; follow notes.

## Known traps

- Loss figures for the winter of 1886-87 are given as precise percentages or
  head counts. Contemporary estimates varied widely and were often guesses;
  give the source and its range. [method]
- One ranch's or one district's losses are generalized to the whole range.
  Keep the figure tied to its place. [method]
- Statehood, territory and boundary dates are mixed up, or an 1880s event is
  placed in "North Dakota" or "Montana" the state. Check which territory and
  which side of the line. [ref: D-25]
- A newspaper's issue date is used as the date of the event it reports.
  [ref: E-15, E-16]
- Vigilante activity is told from one memoir's account and given exact
  numbers. Name the source and label it as recalled. [method]
- Association founding dates and officers are taken from reminiscence. Use
  minutes, charters or period notices. [ref: E-19]
- Railroad facts (when the line reached a place, which stations existed) are
  assumed from later maps. Check a timetable or report for the year. [method]
- Present-day dollar conversions are given without naming the method or
  index. Show the source and the calculation in `notes`, or omit it. [method]
- North Dakota landforms are buttes, not mesas. [house-rule]

## Procedure

1. Pin the claim to a year, a place (territory, county, range) and a subject.
2. Look for a contemporary record first: association minutes, railroad
   report, land or census record, or a period newspaper read in the original.
3. For numbers, find at least two independent contemporary estimates. Report
   the range and its sources rather than one figure.
4. Check territory and boundary for the year.
5. Use secondary works for context, following their notes to the record.
6. Write the narrowest correction, keeping figures tied to their place and
   source.
