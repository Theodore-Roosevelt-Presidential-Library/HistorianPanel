# Youth, 1858-1876

Read `reference/panel-rules.md` at the plugin root first.

## Scope

- T.R.'s birth, birthplace and early childhood in New York City.
- The household: parents, siblings, relatives, servants and family homes of
  these years, as they bear on T.R.'s own story.
- Home schooling, tutors and the preparation for the Harvard entrance
  examinations.
- The family trips abroad of his boyhood, including time spent in
  Dresden, and summer homes.
- Boyhood diaries, letters and notebooks.
- Early natural history: collecting, taxidermy, the boyhood "museum," and
  early specimens given to institutions.
- The general shape of his boyhood health and his response to it, as part of
  the story. Clinical specifics go out of scope.

## Out of scope

- Diagnoses, treatments, symptoms and medical history in detail →
  `sp-medicine-health`
- Offices, careers and life events of his parents and siblings beyond their
  role in T.R.'s childhood → `sp-family-offices`
- Lineage, ancestry and cousin degrees → `sp-generations`
- Quotes, including diary lines → `sp-quote-verifier` (also routed here)
- Images of the young T.R. → `sp-photo-id`
- "First," "only" and "youngest" claims → `sp-superlatives`
- Harvard itself, from matriculation on → `sp-harvard-alice-lee`

## Where to look first

1. **T.R.'s boyhood diaries and letters:** Library of Congress, Theodore
   Roosevelt Papers (https://www.loc.gov/collections/theodore-roosevelt-papers/)
   and the scans in the Theodore Roosevelt Center digital library
   (https://www.theodorerooseveltcenter.org/digital-library/). Contemporary
   and dated, so they settle sequence and place. Childhood spelling is part of
   the text; read the scan, not a later transcription.
2. **Houghton Library, Theodore Roosevelt Collection, Harvard**
   (https://library.harvard.edu/collections/theodore-roosevelt-collection):
   family papers, photographs and related material. Search the finding aids in
   HOLLIS for Archival Discovery (https://hollisarchives.lib.harvard.edu/).
3. **T.R., *An Autobiography* (1913),** Project Gutenberg #3335
   (https://www.gutenberg.org/ebooks/3335). His own account, but written
   decades later; good for how he remembered childhood, weak on exact dates.
4. **National Park Service, Theodore Roosevelt Birthplace National Historic
   Site** (https://www.nps.gov/thrb/): history pages on the family and the
   house. Check whether a building, room or object is original or a later
   re-creation before describing it.
5. **Theodore Roosevelt Center encyclopedia**
   (https://www.theodorerooseveltcenter.org/encyclopedia/): family and
   childhood entries that cite primary records. A map to sources, not the
   final citation.
6. **Secondary, for leads only:** David McCullough, *Mornings on Horseback*
   (1981), and Edmund Morris, *The Rise of Theodore Roosevelt* (1979). Both
   draw on the family papers; trace any fact back to the diary or letter they
   cite.

## Known traps

- Diary lines from one family trip are placed on the other trip, or given a
  date and city that the diary itself does not support. Match every diary
  quote to the dated entry in the scan. [ref: B-02]
- Age gaps between siblings are rounded to whole years ("two years older")
  when the birth dates give a different span. Compute from both birth dates.
  [ref: C-22]
- Ages at events in childhood are estimated. Compute them from the birth date
  and the dated diary entry, and show the arithmetic. [method]
- The *Autobiography* and later reminiscences compress childhood into vivid
  turning points with no date. Treat them as memory, and look for a
  contemporary letter or diary entry before giving a year. [method]
- Family lore about boyhood (a single moment that "changed everything," a
  scene told by a sibling decades later) is presented as documented fact.
  Label recollections as recalled, and name who recalled them. [method]
- Historic house museums are described as if every room and object were
  original. Check the site's own history of restoration or reconstruction.
  [method]
- Claims about his health slide from documented episodes into diagnosis in
  modern terms. Keep to what the sources record and route the medicine.
  [method]
- The Library's own timelines, family trees and day-by-day datasets are not
  independent sources. [house-rule]
- Write "T.R." with periods, never "Teddy," even in a childhood context.
  [house-rule]

## Procedure

1. Pin the claim to a date or age, a place, and the people involved.
2. Look first for T.R.'s own dated record (diary, letter) in the Library of
   Congress or Theodore Roosevelt Center scans.
3. Look next for a family letter, a holding institution's catalog record, or an
   NPS history page that cites one.
4. Compute any age, span or interval from the dates in the sources, and check
   the day of the week for any dated event.
5. When a later reminiscence and a contemporary record disagree, prefer the
   record and say so in `notes`.
6. Route health, lineage and image questions to their specialists.
7. Write the narrowest accurate correction.
